## Q1. Skin tone: stable colour measures, frame-to-frame variance, how much two people differ, and colour histograms for re-ID

### Takeaway
Skin colour measured from uncontrolled images is a weak identity cue. In one study, the same person's face-area lightness varied by about 38–39 units from image to image. That is roughly 3x the average true difference between Black and White subject groups. ITA is known to shift with brightness and yellow chroma. The hue angle h* (from a*/b*) is reported to hold up better under relighting. Within one shot and one camera the variance is probably much smaller, but no source gives video frame-to-frame numbers. Use skin colour only as a per-track, shot-local tie-breaker. Never use it as a veto.

### Cited Findings
- ITA definition: the angle, in degrees, in the CIE-L*a*b* L*–b* plane between the b* axis and the line from (L*=50, b*=0) to the colour (L*, b*). It is usually written ITA = arctan((L*−50)/b*)·180/π. — [Emergent Mind ITA overview](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/individual-typology-angle-ita) (secondary summary)
- ITA is sensitive to illumination, "especially to brightness or lack of yellow chroma". ITA values from different studies are only comparable when lighting and devices are known and corrected. There is "no consensus for ITA binning thresholds". One common scheme puts Type 1 above 55° and Type 6 at 10° or below. — [Kalb et al. 2023, Revisiting Skin Tone Fairness in Dermatological Lesion Classification](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.09640) (via search summary; full text blocked)
- ITA "is defined for any colour, but only meaningful for skin". Including hair, artefacts or dark borders in the region gives misleading results, so the measurement region must be masked to skin. — [Kalb et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.09640) (via search summary)
- In a synthetic study with 18 lighting conditions, patch-based ITA showed a consistent linear bias relative to segmentation-based ITA under most lighting. The authors corrected it with a regression calibration. — [Skin Color Measurement from Dermatoscopic Images: An Evaluation on a Synthetic Dataset](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04494v2) (via search summary)
- With protocol control of geometry, lighting and exposure, smartphone ITA can agree with lab tristimulus colorimeters within ±1°. Uncontrolled conditions break this. — [Emergent Mind ITA overview](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/individual-typology-angle-ita) (secondary; underlying study not identified)
- Thong, Joniak & Xiang (Sony AI, ICCV 2023) propose a two-dimensional apparent skin colour: perceptual lightness L* for tone and hue angle h* (red to yellow) for hue. They report that the hue angle is robust to face relighting, so it carries information complementary to L*. — [Beyond Skin Tone (ICCV 2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05148); [Sony AI blog](https://ai.sony/blog/beyond-skin-tone-a-multidimensional-measure-of-apparent-skin-color) (via search summary)
- Howard, Sirotin, Tipton & Vemury (IEEE T-BIOM 2021) found that intra-subject face-area lightness measures (FALM) from photos ranged, on average, by 38 units for Black or African American subjects and 39 for White subjects. That is more than a 2-fold difference in measured lightness for one subject from image to image. Per the related patent text, it is "three times more than the average difference in ground-truth FALMs observed between the two race groups". Under controlled conditions, image FALM was a good estimate of colorimeter FALM. Their images came from multiple acquisition devices, not consecutive video frames. — [Reliability and Validity of Image-Based and Self-Reported Skin Phenotype Metrics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11240); [US patent 12,333,765 text](https://patents.justia.com/patent/12333765) (via search summary)
- An automated ITA-based skin tone rater agreed with the manual consensus at ≥96% when "same or up to one skin tone of difference" was allowed. Human observers rating the same image set were inconsistent with each other. — [Krishnapriya, King & Bowyer, WACVW 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2022W/DVPB/papers/Krishnapriya_Analysis_of_Manual_and_Automated_Skin_Tone_Assignments_WACVW_2022_paper.pdf) (via search summary)
- SREDS, a dichromatic-separation skin colour measure, beat ITA-type measures in both controlled and varying illumination. All measures showed higher intra-subject variability under varying illumination. — [SREDS (arXiv 2104.02926)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.02926) (via search summary)
- A 2026 study, Limitations of ITA for Skin Type Estimation Under Uncontrolled Imaging Conditions, frames ITA as a feature rather than a classifier and documents its limits under uncontrolled capture. No numbers were retrievable. — [Alipour 2026, Skin Research and Technology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/srt.70374)
- Colour histograms in tracking: one multi-target tracker compares each target's first-appearance colour histogram (16×16×16 bins) with the current one using the Bhattacharyya distance d = sqrt(1 − BC), where BC = Σ sqrt(p_i·q_i). — [arXiv 1511.01726 (MTT with variational Bayesian clusters)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.01726) (via search summary)
- A sports-video tracker uses HSV with 10 bins per channel in an N_H×N_S + N_V layout. The layout keeps chroma (H, S) separate from shading (V) so shading changes affect only the V part. — [arXiv 1612.06454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.06454) (via search summary)
- No source found gives a standard Bhattacharyya acceptance threshold for person matching. Thresholds are tuned per dataset from same-person vs different-person distance distributions. — [search result set incl. arXiv 1511.01726 / 1612.06454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.01726)
- Colour names for re-ID: Salient Color Names (SCNCD; Yang et al., ECCV 2014) uses 16 colour names from the 16-colour RGB palette with soft assignment ("higher probability ... to the color name nearer to the color"), fused over several colour spaces. It was evaluated on VIPeR and PRID 450S; exact numbers were not retrieved. — [Springer PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-319-10590-1_35.pdf); [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/eccv/2014/yang2014eccv-salient)

### Inferences
- A practical stable measure (inference, not from a single source): convert the frame to CIE-Lab with a fixed sRGB/D65 transform. Sample two cheek patches and a forehead patch placed from 68-point landmarks. Each patch should be about 0.15 × inter-ocular distance square, kept away from eyes, brows, lips, nostrils and the hairline. Reject pixels with L* < 15 or > 95 (shadow and specular highlight) and pixels far from the per-patch median (e.g., > 2.5·MAD). Take the per-frame median (L*, a*, b*) and keep per-track running medians of L* and h* = atan2(b*, a*). Cost is well under 1 ms per face on CPU (a few hundred pixels).
- In a kiss or hug, the cheek touching the partner is unreliable: it may be occluded by the partner's skin or by the partner's hair, and inter-reflection tints it. Sample only the far-side cheek and the forehead, and drop a frame's sample when the patch falls inside the other face's box or mask.
- Calibrate per shot, not globally. Let each track's within-shot spread (MAD of L* and h* over clean, non-contact frames) be σ_A and σ_B. Use skin colour only if |median_A − median_B| > 3·max(σ_A, σ_B) on that shot. Given the Howard et al. cross-image numbers, cross-shot comparisons (after a cut, new lighting) should not use absolute skin values. At most, re-learn the per-shot means from the first clean frames.
- Prefer hue angle h* and chroma over L*/ITA for lighting robustness (Thong et al.). Both shift under white-balance changes, though, so camera auto-white-balance drift within a shot is a confound. Normalising by a reference region, such as the scene mean a*/b* or a gray-world estimate per frame, is a cheap correction, but it was not validated in any source found.
- Couples of similar skin tone (same ethnicity, similar tan) are common. The cue will often be uninformative, so the fusion must degrade to "no information" (weight → 0) rather than force a decision.

### Gaps
- No source found measures frame-to-frame (same camera, same shot) variance of L*, a*, b*, ITA or h* on video faces. This needs measuring on the user's own clips; record per-track MAD on non-contact frames.
- No published distribution of pairwise skin-colour distances (ΔE, ΔITA, Δh*) between two random people in video was found.
- The Chardon ITA category boundaries (>55 very light, 41–55 light, 28–41 intermediate, 10–28 tan, −30–10 brown, < −30 dark) are widely quoted but were not verified in this session. Only "Type 1 > 55°, Type 6 ≤ 10°" was confirmed, from one scheme in Kalb et al.
- No published threshold for HSV-histogram/Bhattacharyya person matching was found.

## Q2. Gender/age classifiers (InsightFace genderage, FairFace, MiVOLO): accuracy, failure modes, flicker, temporal aggregation, bias

### Takeaway
On CPU there are two ONNX options. InsightFace genderage.onnx is about 1.3 MB, uses a bounding-box crop without landmark alignment, and has no official accuracy figure; one third-party table suggests roughly 85% gender accuracy for an InsightFace small model. FaceFusion's fairface.onnx is a FairFace classifier with gender, 9 age bins and 7 races, using a landmark-aligned 224×224 crop. FairFace-trained gender accuracy is about 94–96% in published benchmarks, with under 1% gap between groups for gender, but those are frontal, uncontaminated faces. MiVOLO is more accurate (97–99% gender) but is a transformer with poor ONNX support. Use gender only as a per-track aggregated prior, with log-odds accumulated over quality-weighted frames, and only when both tracks have confident and different gender estimates.

### Cited Findings
- InsightFace Attribute (genderage) code: it is treated as gender/age when the output has 3 values. Gender = argmax over the first two values; age = round(third value × 100). The crop is bounding-box centred with scale = input_size / (max(w, h) × 1.5) and rotation fixed at 0, so there is no landmark alignment. Pixel mean is 127.5 and std 128.0 unless MXNet-style preprocessing nodes are found (then 0/1), with swapRB = True. The input size is read from the model; per the brief it is 96×96 for buffalo_l. — [insightface attribute.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/deepinsight/insightface/master/python-package/insightface/model_zoo/attribute.py)
- genderage.onnx is about 1.26–1.32 MB in the buffalo_l pack, much smaller than the detector or recognizer. No official accuracy figure for it was found. — [search summary of HF buffalo_l listings](https://huggingface.co/public-data/insightface/tree/main/models/buffalo_l); [InsightFace model zoo README](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface/blob/e68b8b076fb11e9f75c87244d234385f0f318181/model_zoo/README.md)
- A third-party table (Savchenko 2021, lightweight multi-task networks) lists an InsightFace "New" model at about 84.7% gender accuracy, age MAE 8.44 and about 0.3 MB. It lists InsightFace ResNet-50 at 87.52% gender accuracy and age MAE 8.57. Which file this row corresponds to, and the test dataset, were not confirmed. — [arXiv 2103.17107](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.17107) (via search summary; indicative only)
- A Rust port notes that buffalo_l genderage.onnx expects BGR, 0–255 input. Wrong channel order or normalisation silently degrades predictions. — [face_id crate gender_age.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/face_id/0.4.1/source/src/gender_age.rs) (via search summary)
- FairFace dataset: 108,501 images from YFCC-100M, balanced over 7 race groups (White, Black, Indian, East Asian, Southeast Asian, Middle Eastern, Latino), labelled for race, gender and age group (WACV 2021). — [FairFace arXiv 1908.04913](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04913) (via search summary)
- The FairFace-trained model achieved "less than 1% accuracy discrepancy between male and female and White and non-White for gender classification". The gender performance gap was largest for the LFWA+-trained model (32%). — [FairFace paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.04913) (via search summary; table cells not retrieved)
- A secondary source gives overall cross-dataset gender accuracy of FairFace-trained 95.7%, UTKFace-trained 90.4%, LFWA+-trained 83.8% and CelebA-trained 87.0%. Unverified against the paper. Papers-with-Code lists FairFace-benchmark top-1 of 94.2 for gender, 59.7 for age and 93.7 for race; the race setting (7-class vs 4-class) is unclear. — [Emergent Mind FairFace page](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/fairface-dataset); [paperswithcode mirror](https://paperswithcode.co/paper/1908.04913) (secondary)
- FairFace repo: faces are detected and aligned with dlib get_face_chip() with padding 0.25 (1.25 for the commercial-API bias experiment). Outputs are race-7 (White, Black, Latino_Hispanic, East Asian, Southeast Asian, Indian, Middle Eastern), race-4 (White, Black, Asian, Indian), gender (Male, Female) and 9 age bins (0-2, 3-9, 10-19, 20-29, 30-39, 40-49, 50-59, 60-69, 70+). Images and labels are CC BY 4.0; the model license is not stated. — [dchen236/FairFace](https://github.com/dchen236/FairFace)
- FaceFusion's classifier is "fairface" (vendor dchen236, CC-BY-4.0, 2021) as a single ONNX file. It uses a 5-point landmark warp to the arcface_112_v2 template at 224×224 with ImageNet mean/std (0.485, 0.456, 0.406)/(0.229, 0.224, 0.225), RGB. The model's outputs are integer class ids (race_id, gender_id, age_id). Mapping: gender 1 = female, else male. Race 0 = white, 1 = black, 2 = latino, 3 and 4 = asian (East and Southeast merged), 5 = indian, 6 = arabic. Age 0→range(0,2), 1→range(3,9), 2→range(10,19) … 8→range(70,100); Python ranges exclude their upper end, so e.g. age 2 and 9 fall outside every bin. Classification runs for every detected face in create_faces(). — [facefusion/face_classifier.py @3.9.2](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/3.9.2/facefusion/face_classifier.py); [face_creator.py @3.9.2](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/3.9.2/facefusion/face_creator.py)
- MiVOLO (GitHub README) results:
  - volo_d1 face-only age+gender on IMDB-clean: MAE 4.22, CS@5 68.68, gender 99.38%.
  - mivolo_d1 face+body on IMDB-clean: MAE 4.24, gender 99.46%; body only: MAE 6.87, gender 96.48%.
  - UTKFace face-only: MAE 4.23, gender 97.69%.
  - Lagenda face+body: mivolo_d1 MAE 3.99, gender 97.36%; mivolov2_d1_384 MAE 3.65, gender 97.99%.
  - Lagenda→FairFace: age accuracy 61.07%, gender 95.73%.
  - Lagenda→Adience: gender 96.51% (v1), 97.39% (v2).
  - Practical notes: ONNX export is "technically feasible" but "not advisable" (poor batch performance), TensorRT/OpenVINO export is not possible (missing col2im), the detector is YOLOv8x person+face, and the license is Apache-2.0 per the repo sidebar.
  — [WildChlamydia/MiVOLO](https://github.com/WildChlamydia/MiVOLO)
- Human annotators on 3,000 IMDB-clean images had an age MAE of 7.22 years (reported in the MiVOLO paper per a search summary). Single-image age is therefore too noisy for identity unless two people differ by decades. — [MiVOLO arXiv 2307.04616](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04616) (via search summary)
- Bias: in Gender Shades (Buolamwini & Gebru, FAT* 2018), across three commercial gender classifiers, the highest error for darker-skinned females was 34.7% and the highest for lighter-skinned males was 0.8%. Both are worst cases across systems, the benchmark was balanced by gender and Fitzpatrick skin type, and the systems date from 2018. — [PMLR v81](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v81/buolamwini18a.html); [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/gender-shades-intersectional-accuracy-disparities-in-commercial-gender-classification/) (via search summary)
- A 2025 audit reports TPR by race for gender classifiers. Black-female TPR was 0.650 for a FairFace-trained model vs 0.550 for a UTKFace-trained model; Black disparate impact was 0.887 vs 0.751. Balanced training data narrows but does not close the gap. — [Auditing and Mitigating Bias in Gender Classification Algorithms (arXiv 2510.17873)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17873) (via search summary)
- Temporal aggregation in video:
  - Demirkus et al. (CVPRW 2010) carry a Bayesian posterior through frames for gender in unconstrained video and report about 90% accuracy by the last frame. — [mlanthology CVPRW 2010](https://mlanthology.org/cvprw/2010/demirkus2010cvprw-gender) (via search summary)
  - Becerra-Riera et al. (ICPR) choose frames with an attribute-specific quality model, classify them with MobileNet and take a majority vote. They report better accuracy, fewer frames and reduced minority-class bias; no numbers were retrieved. — [ICPR slides](https://ailb-web.ing.unimore.it/icpr/media/slides/11575.pdf); [EURECOM entry](https://www.eurecom.edu/fr/node/4901688) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- Crop contamination in contact: InsightFace genderage uses a 1.5× bounding-box crop with no rotation. When two faces touch, the crop will contain a large part of the partner's face, and tilted kissing heads stay rotated. FaceFusion's fairface crop is landmark-aligned and tighter, so it is likely less affected, but landmarks themselves fail under heavy overlap. Gender/age readings during the contact frames should be discarded or heavily down-weighted. Learn each track's attributes on non-contact frames before and after.
- Recommended aggregation (inference): per track, accumulate a clipped log-odds L = Σ w_t·clip(logit(p_female,t), −2, +2), with weight w_t = detector score × frontalness (|yaw| < 30°) × no-overlap flag. Declare a track's gender only when |L|/Σw_t > 1.0 (mean p ≥ 0.73) and Σw_t ≥ 10 good frames. If the model outputs only an argmax (as fairface.onnx in FaceFusion does), use the vote fraction instead and require ≥ 80% agreement over ≥ 10 good frames.
- Apply the gender cue only when both tracks are confidently labelled and the labels differ. When they agree or either is uncertain, set its weight to 0. This avoids hurting same-gender couples and the groups with higher misclassification rates (per Gender Shades and the audit above).
- Age: with MAE around 4–8 years per image and 7.22 years for humans, use age only when the per-track medians differ by more than about 20 years (adult with child or elderly person). Otherwise treat it as uninformative.
- Race labels (FaceFusion's 6 coarse classes) should not be used as an identity cue. They duplicate the weaker skin-colour cue, carry known group-dependent error, and add ethical and legal risk. Per-track appearance colour already captures any useful signal.
- Running fairface.onnx (ResNet-34-class, 224×224) per face per frame on CPU likely costs tens of ms. Running it once per track on 10–20 selected good frames fits the "per track, not per frame" budget. InsightFace genderage (about 1.3 MB) is millisecond-class per face.

### Gaps
- No official accuracy for InsightFace genderage.onnx was found, and no study of its per-frame flip rate in video.
- No published frame-to-frame gender-flip rates were found for any of these classifiers. They should be measured on the user's clips as the fraction of frames whose argmax disagrees with the track majority, split by contact and non-contact frames.
- The exact FairFace cross-dataset tables (per-race gender accuracy) and the architecture of the facefusion fairface.onnx were not retrievable (arxiv/thecvf blocked). The ONNX graph should be inspected locally to confirm the architecture and that the outputs are argmax ids.
- No published evaluation of these classifiers on profile views or on kissing/hugging occlusion was found. Makeup and child failure modes are mentioned generally but no numbers were retrieved.

## Q3. Hair and clothing colour (regions above or below the face) as identity cues in a hug

### Takeaway
Clothing below the face is a well-established complementary cue for person identity in photo collections. Gallagher & Chen 2008 used the region beneath the face, and PIPA/PIPER combined body, poselet and face cues because only 52% of PIPA instances had a usable frontal face. In a hug, though, torsos interpenetrate and arms cross. A torso box scaled to face size is therefore only reliable before and after contact, and should be learned there and gated by overlap during contact.

### Cited Findings
- Gallagher & Chen, "Clothing cosegmentation for recognizing people" (CVPR 2008): they learn a global clothing mask from pixel statistics near (below) the face and segment clothing per image with graph cuts using a model learned from images believed to show the same person in the same clothing. Clothing segmentation "noticeably improves recognition accuracy". Later work credits them with using superpixels beneath a face detection. Region sizes and accuracy numbers were not retrieved. — [mlanthology CVPR 2008](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/2008/gallagher2008cvpr-clothing); [typeset summary](https://typeset.io/papers/clothing-cosegmentation-for-recognizing-people-39we9jda38); [KIT thesis](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000026342/2021022) (via search summary)
- PIPA (Zhang et al., CVPR 2015, "Beyond Frontal Faces"): only about 52% of person instances have a high-resolution frontal face usable by standard recognizers. PIPER combines poselet-level person recognizers (deep CNN), a face recognizer and a global recognizer, and significantly improves on DeepFace in the unconstrained setup. Overall PIPA accuracy is 83.05% according to a secondary summary (unverified). — [arXiv 1501.05703](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.05703); [CVF html](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2015/html/Zhang_Beyond_Frontal_Faces_2015_CVPR_paper.html); [Liner summary](https://liner.com/review/beyond-frontal-faces-improving-person-recognition-using-multiple-cues) (via search summary)
- A US patent describes clustering images using a personal clothing model, i.e. clothing as an identity cue tied to a face. — [USPTO 8861873](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8861873)
- Colour-histogram building blocks (HSV with separate V; Bhattacharyya distance; 16×16×16 bins) are listed in Q1. — [arXiv 1612.06454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.06454); [arXiv 1511.01726](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.01726)

### Inferences
- Torso box (inference; standard practice in the clothing-cue literature, but these exact numbers were not taken from a source): with face box width w, height h and centre x_c, use torso x ∈ [x_c − 1.0w, x_c + 1.0w] and y ∈ [y_bottom + 0.5h, y_bottom + 2.5h]. Clip to the frame and to a person-segmentation mask if one is available. Hair region: a band about 0.3–0.6h above the brow line within the face width, minus the skin mask. Descriptor: HS 8×8 + V 8 histogram (separating shading) or 11/16 soft colour names. Compare with the Bhattacharyya distance against a per-track model built on non-contact frames.
- In a hug or kiss: (a) torsos and arms overlap, and the person in front occludes the other's clothing; (b) hands and arms (skin) enter the torso box; (c) couples often wear similar colours (black and white are very common); (d) hair often spills across the partner's face and shoulder in a kiss. Gate the cue off for any frame where the two torso boxes overlap by IoU > 0.1 or the face boxes touch. Learn each track's clothing model only from frames where its torso box is unobstructed. Set the cue weight to 0 when the two tracks' clothing models are close, e.g. Bhattacharyya distance < 0.3 between the A and B models (a starting guess to tune).
- Clothing and hair change across cuts and scenes, unlike faces. They are useful within a shot (tracks before and after contact), not for cross-shot identity unless it is the same scene.
- Cost: histograms over a few thousand pixels take sub-millisecond time on CPU, which fits the millisecond-class budget.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies clothing-colour reliability specifically during hugs or kisses, or in two-person close-contact video.
- The exact Gallagher & Chen torso geometry and accuracy figures and the PIPER per-cue ablations could not be retrieved (full texts blocked).

## Q4. How the open face-swap tools select faces: FaceFusion modes, thresholds and defaults, and roop / roop-unleashed / Deep-Live-Cam / Rope "map faces"

### Takeaway
None of the open tools does joint one-to-one assignment, such as Hungarian matching with an exclusivity constraint between people. FaceFusion applies one averaged source face to every target face that passes the filters, or every target within a cosine-distance threshold of a single reference face. Its gender, race and age selectors are hard filters, its default threshold is equivalent to cosine similarity > 0.4, and since 3.7 it has an optional IoU tracker. Deep-Live-Cam clusters target embeddings with k-means (elbow) and assigns each face to the nearest centroid with no threshold and no uniqueness rule. Rope gives each detection the best-similarity slot above a threshold (default 55, i.e. cosine ≥ 0.10), again without exclusivity. Roop and roop-unleashed use a first-match-under-threshold rule. So when two faces overlap and embeddings degrade, these tools can give both faces the same source or skip one; none uses attributes softly.

### Cited Findings
FaceFusion (verified in source, tag 3.9.2, Oct 2026)
- select_faces(reference_frame, source_frames, target_frames) in facefusion/face_selector.py:
  - Mode 'many' returns all target faces after sort_and_filter_faces.
  - Mode 'one' returns the first face after sort and filter.
  - Mode 'reference' detects faces in the reference frame, applies sort and filter, picks get_one_face(reference_faces, reference_face_position), then returns every target face that passes compare_faces against that single reference.
  — [face_selector.py @3.9.2](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/3.9.2/facefusion/face_selector.py)
- Distance (3.3.0 through 3.9.2): calculate_face_distance = 1 − dot(embedding_norm_a, embedding_norm_b), i.e. the cosine distance on L2-normalised ArcFace embeddings. compare_faces maps it from [0, 2] to [0, 1] with numpy.interp and accepts when the result is < reference_face_distance. — [face_selector.py @3.9.2](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/3.9.2/facefusion/face_selector.py)
- Defaults (3.9.2, program.py):
  - face_selector_mode 'reference'; face_selector_order 'large-small'
  - reference_face_distance 0.3 (range 0.0–1.0, step 0.05); reference_face_position 0; reference_frame_number 0
  - face_selector_gender, face_selector_race, age_start and age_end unset
  - face_tracker_score 0.0, i.e. tracking off (range 0.0–0.5, step 0.05)
  - face_detector_model 'yolo_face'; face_detector_score 0.5; face_landmarker_score 0.5; target_frame_amount 2 (range 0–10)
  — [program.py @3.9.2](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/3.9.2/facefusion/program.py); [choices.py @3.9.2](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/3.9.2/facefusion/choices.py)
- History: in tag 3.0.0, compare_faces used the raw cosine distance (no interp), the attribute was normed_embedding, and the default reference_face_distance was 0.6. Tag 3.3.0 introduced the [0, 2]→[0, 1] interp and changed the default to 0.3. ### Inferences
- Thresholds converted to cosine similarity (arithmetic from the code above):
  - FaceFusion 3.3+: (1 − cos)/2 < 0.3 ⇔ cos > 0.40 (3.0: 1 − cos < 0.6, the same).
  - roop: ‖a − b‖² = 2 − 2cos < 0.85 ⇔ cos > 0.575.
  - Rope: 100 − 50(1 − cos) ≥ 55 ⇔ cos ≥ 0.10, which is very permissive.
  - roop-unleashed: if compute_cosine_distance = 1 − cos (unverified), ≤ 0.65 ⇔ cos ≥ 0.35.
- Why these tools fail in kisses and hugs:
  - (1) Independent per-face matching with no exclusivity, so both faces can get the same source.
  - (2) Detector NMS (IoU 0.4 in FaceFusion) can suppress one of two overlapping faces.
  - (3) Embeddings of an occluded or contaminated crop fall below threshold, so the face is skipped and the swap flickers, or they fall closer to the other person's reference, so identities swap.
  - (4) Hard gender/race/age filters remove a face entirely on a single misclassification.
  - (5) FaceFusion's tracker matches greedily by IoU, so when two faces overlap, the box IoU between the two people's tracks is high and the tracks can swap.
- In FaceFusion, swapping two people with two different sources takes two passes (reference A → source A, then reference B → source B on the output), because one run applies one averaged source to all selected faces. Each pass is independent, so nothing stops both passes from selecting the same face in a contact frame. This workflow is inferred from the code; it was not documented on docs.facefusion.io in this session.
- What to borrow: FaceFusion's refill_faces idea (interpolate geometry across gaps, copy identity from the anchor) is directly relevant to contact frames. Identity should be carried from clean anchor frames, not re-estimated from contaminated crops.

## Q5. Combining attribute cues with embeddings and motion: fusion schemes, veto rules, and when attributes hurt

### Takeaway
The best-documented fusion pattern in tracking is gating plus weighted cost plus joint assignment. DeepSORT gates candidate pairs by Mahalanobis motion distance (χ² 95% = 9.4877 for 4 DoF) and by appearance cosine distance (default 0.2), sets gated pairs to an infinite cost and solves the assignment jointly. For gender in video, Bayesian posterior accumulation (Demirkus 2010) and quality-selected majority voting (Becerra-Riera) are documented. Attribute cues hurt when used as hard filters on per-frame predictions, as FaceFusion's selectors are. They should enter as bounded, per-track log-likelihood terms with zero weight when uninformative.

### Cited Findings
- DeepSORT (nwojke/deep_sort) defaults:
  - Motion gate: chi2inv95[4] = 9.4877 on the Kalman Mahalanobis distance.
  - Appearance gate: max_cosine_distance = 0.2 (CLI default, "Gating threshold for cosine distance metric (object appearance)").
  - Gated entries set to INFTY_COST = 1e+5 before linear assignment.
  - Tracker defaults: max_iou_distance = 0.7, max_age = 30, n_init = 3; detection min_confidence default 0.8; nms_max_overlap 1.0; nn_budget None.
  — [deep_sort/linear_assignment.py](https://github.com/nwojke/deep_sort/blob/master/deep_sort/linear_assignment.py); [deep_sort/kalman_filter.py](https://github.com/nwojke/deep_sort/blob/master/deep_sort/kalman_filter.py); [deep_sort/tracker.py](https://github.com/nwojke/deep_sort/blob/master/deep_sort/tracker.py); [deep_sort_app.py](https://github.com/nwojke/deep_sort/blob/master/deep_sort_app.py)
- Bayesian temporal accumulation of gender posteriors in unconstrained video reached about 90% by the last frame (Demirkus et al., CVPRW 2010). — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/cvprw/2010/demirkus2010cvprw-gender) (via search summary)
- Quality-selected frames plus majority vote improves video demographic estimation and reduces minority-class bias (Becerra-Riera et al.). — [ICPR slides](https://ailb-web.ing.unimore.it/icpr/media/slides/11575.pdf) (via search summary)
- Multi-cue person recognition (face + body/poselets + global) outperforms face-only when faces are not frontal (PIPER on PIPA). — [Zhang et al. CVPR 2015](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2015/html/Zhang_Beyond_Frontal_Faces_2015_CVPR_paper.html) (via search summary)
- Evidence that hard attribute filters drop faces: in FaceFusion, filter_faces_by_gender, filter_faces_by_race and filter_faces_by_age exclude any face whose single-frame label mismatches. With 'auto', the label of the source face sets the filter, so a misclassified source removes every correct target. — [face_selector.py @3.9.2](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/3.9.2/facefusion/face_selector.py)
- Evidence that ungated, non-exclusive matching causes identity hopping: Rope's own threshold help text says to raise it "to reduce faces hopping around when swapping multiple people". — [Rope _default_data.py](https://github.com/Hillobar/Rope/blob/bf17d2305b127dde1c55ffe301c4ee0f32ae220b/rope/qt/_default_data.py)
- Skin-colour cue magnitude: intra-subject variation across images was about 3x the average between-group true difference (Howard et al.). An unnormalised skin cue can mislead more than it helps across images. — [arXiv 2106.11240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11240) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- Recommended fusion (inference, built from the cited patterns): for the two tracks {A, B} and the two detections {d1, d2} in a contact frame, compute
  `cost(track, det) = w_emb·(1 − cos(e_det, μ_track)) + w_mot·min(D²_Mahalanobis / 9.49, 3) + Σ_k w_k·clip(−log P_k(det | track), 0, c_k)`,
  where k ∈ {skin h*/L*, clothing histogram, hair histogram, gender, age}. Solve the 2×2 (or n×n) assignment jointly (Hungarian, or compare the two permutations for n = 2) so each detection goes to exactly one track.
  - Normalise embedding cosine to the embedding's own scale: for ArcFace w600k_r50, the ~0.4 FaceFusion threshold is a reference point. Weight each attribute term by its reliability on this shot: w_k = 0 unless the A-vs-B separation of cue k exceeds 3× its within-track spread (Q1 and Q3). Clip each attribute term (c_k ≈ 1–2 nats) so no single attribute can override a strong embedding plus motion agreement.
  - Do the decision as a log-likelihood ratio between the two permutations, LLR = cost(A→d2, B→d1) − cost(A→d1, B→d2). Commit only if |LLR| exceeds a margin. Otherwise carry the previous binding (hysteresis) or carry identity forward like FaceFusion's refill.
- Veto rules only for physically certain constraints: (i) a detection cannot belong to both tracks (exclusivity); (ii) motion gate χ² 9.49 (4 DoF) / 13.28 (99%, 4 DoF) when motion is smooth. Never use per-frame gender, age, race or skin as vetoes. Attributes are learned per track on clean frames and enter only as bounded soft terms.
- Attributes are most helpful exactly where embeddings fail (profile, occlusion in contact) and most harmful when computed on those same contaminated frames. So compute attribute models from non-contact frames, then evaluate per-frame attribute likelihoods only on parts known to be unoccluded: the far cheek, the forehead, torso areas outside the overlap.
- Cost budget: skin patches and colour histograms are sub-millisecond per face per frame on CPU. InsightFace genderage is about 1–3 ms per face per frame. fairface or MiVOLO should be run once per track on 10–20 selected frames.

### Gaps
- No published system was found that fuses skin colour, gender or clothing with face embeddings specifically for close-contact or kissing video. All the fusion weights above are proposals to tune on the test bench.
- No study found quantifies when attribute cues reduce identity-assignment accuracy relative to embedding plus motion alone. This should be measured on the user's kiss clips by ablation: embedding+motion vs +skin vs +clothing vs +gender, reporting identity-switch counts.
- The 99% χ² value for 4 DoF (13.28) is standard statistics, not taken from deep_sort, which only lists the 95% table.
