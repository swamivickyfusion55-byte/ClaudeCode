# Pixel separation of touching / overlapping faces (cut-to-cut ownership, front/behind order, CPU)

Scope: two faces in contact (kiss, cheek to cheek, hug with faces pressed together) in a face-swap compositor that already has, per face, 106 landmarks, a 256-px aligned crop, an XSeg occluder mask (xseg_2.onnx), optional BiSeNet parsing (19 classes) and an exclusive ownership rule (front face cut out of the back face along its landmark outline). Goal: a better seam where the faces meet and better front/behind evidence, on a 2-vCPU CPU with ONNX.

Note on sources: several hosts were blocked in this session (docs.ultralytics.com, scikit-image.org, ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org, huggingface.co and github.com binary downloads), so some numbers come from GitHub-hosted READMEs, search summaries, or a local benchmark run in this session. Every number is tagged with where it came from; "[unverified]" marks recall or estimates that were not confirmed against a source here.

## 1. Colour-model and graph-based separation (GMM likelihood ratio, GrabCut, watershed, random walker, geodesic), similar skin tones, runtimes, edge-aware placement

### Takeaway
On 2 CPU cores, marker-controlled watershed on an edge map (about 0.2 to 1.2 ms), Euclidean distance splits (about 0.6 to 2.5 ms), guided-filter refinement (about 1.6 to 7 ms) and DIS flow (0.4 to 4.5 ms) fit a per-frame budget. GrabCut (24 to 390 ms), random walker (27 to 118 ms) and geodesic MCP splits (117 to 520 ms) only fit on contact frames and only on a cropped band. Per-person colour GMMs are cheap to score but cost about 35 to 45 ms to fit, so fit them once per shot or every N frames from uncontested cheek and forehead pixels. Colour alone cannot be trusted when the two people have similar skin. The seam should be driven by the occluding edge plus a landmark shape prior, with colour as a weak tie-breaker.

### Cited Findings
- OpenCV `cv2.grabCut(img, mask, rect, bgdModel, fgdModel, iterCount[, mode])` takes an 8-bit 3-channel image and an 8-bit mask of GrabCut classes (GC_BGD, GC_FGD, GC_PR_BGD, GC_PR_FGD). It can be initialised from a mask instead of a rectangle (`GC_INIT_WITH_MASK`), so landmark-certain regions of face A can be hard foreground and face B's certain region hard background. — Local docstring of `cv2.grabCut` in OpenCV 4.10.0 ([OpenCV grabCut docs](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d3/d47/group__imgproc__segmentation.html))
- Measured grabCut cost on 2 cores: 24.1 / 47.1 / 86.8 ms at 256 px (1 / 3 / 5 iterations), 72.9 / 118.7 / 174.9 ms at 384 px, 107.5 / 223.5 / 390.1 ms at 512 px. — Local bench (see table)
- Measured `cv2.watershed` with three markers: 0.16 ms at 256 px, 0.41 ms at 384 px, 1.15 ms at 512 px. — Local bench
- scikit-image `random_walker(data, labels, beta=130, mode='cg_j', tol=0.001, copy=True, return_full_prob=False, spacing=None, *, prob_tol=0.001, channel_axis=None)`. "beta: penalization coefficient for the random walker motion (the greater beta, the more difficult the diffusion)". Modes: 'bf' (LU, "fast for small images (<1024x1024)"), 'cg', 'cg_j' (Jacobi-preconditioned CG), 'cg_mg' (multigrid, needs pyamg). `return_full_prob=True` returns per-label probabilities, which can serve as a soft ownership map. Notes: "Multichannel inputs are scaled with all channel data combined. Ensure all channels are separately normalized." — Local docstring, scikit-image 0.26.0 ([skimage.segmentation docs](https://scikit-image.org/docs/stable/api/skimage.segmentation.html); host blocked here, read from the installed package)
- Measured random_walker (Lab, beta=130, cg_j): 27.2 ms at 256 px, 70.1 ms at 384 px, 118.2 ms at 512 px; 'bf' at 256 px was 28.3 ms. — Local bench
- scikit-image `watershed` supports `compactness` ("compact watershed... higher values result in more regularly-shaped watershed basins") and `watershed_line`. The docstring warns that the watershed line "may not catch borders between adjacent marker regions". — Local docstring, scikit-image 0.26.0
- Per-person GMMs (3 full-covariance components in Lab, about 4k training pixels per person): fit plus log-likelihood ratio on 4.6k to 19.7k band pixels took about 36 to 38 ms with sklearn (one cold-start run took 195 ms). cv2.ml.EM fitting of the same two models took 37 to 43 ms. — Local bench
- A geodesic split (two `skimage.graph.MCP_Geometric` cost propagations on a gradient-based cost) took 117 ms at 256 px and 520 ms at 512 px. A Euclidean split from two `cv2.distanceTransform` calls took 0.57 to 2.48 ms. — Local bench
- `cv2.ximgproc.guidedFilter` (radius 4, colour guide) took 1.64 ms at 256 px and 7.03 ms at 512 px. A Scharr gradient magnitude took 0.13 to 0.54 ms. — Local bench
- FaceFusion's own masks already apply a soft remap after blurring, `(cv2.GaussianBlur(...).clip(0.5, 1) - 0.5) * 2`, which keeps the 0.5 iso-line where it was and steepens the feather. The same trick can be used on an ownership map to keep a seam exclusive while removing aliasing. — [FaceFusion face_masker.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facefusion/facefusion/master/facefusion/face_masker.py)
- Pixel-level occlusion-boundary detectors exist that output both the boundary and which side is in front: DOOBNet (single-stream encoder-decoder, trained on PIOD, about 10k PASCAL VOC images, plus BSDS ownership) and P2ORM (ECCV 2020, a pixel-pair occlusion relationship map that encodes boundary location and orientation and ignores semantics; also used to refine monocular depth maps). — [DOOBNet arXiv 1806.03772](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.03772); [P2ORM arXiv 2007.12088](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.12088.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Why colour fails for similar skin.** The two people's skin distributions overlap heavily when tones are similar, and lighting moves pixels within a face (lit side vs shadow side) by more than the difference between the two people. A per-person GMM learned from one side's cheek then wrongly claims the other person's pixels that have the same lighting. In a kiss or cheek-to-cheek pose the contact zone is usually in shadow or partial shadow for both people, which is exactly where the models are weakest. Use the likelihood ratio only when it is decisive (for example |mean LLR| > 2 nats over a region), and otherwise fall back to edges plus shape. Colour does help when there is a clear contrast: different skin tones, a beard or stubble vs smooth skin, make-up or lipstick on one person, or hair from one person crossing the other's face.
- **Cheapest robust per-frame seam (recommended default).** Work in a contact ROI, for example the bounding box of the overlap of the two dilated landmark hulls plus a margin, resampled to about 192 to 256 px.
  1. Markers: A-certain = erode(hullA ∩ XSegA, k) minus dilate(hullB, k), and the mirror for B. Use k of about 4 to 6 px at 256-px scale. Add a third marker for "neither" (outside both dilated hulls). Do not seed lips, teeth or eyes as colour samples, but they can stay as markers.
  2. Relief image for watershed: R = edge(x) · w(x), where edge = normalised Scharr magnitude (or a structured-forest edge map, see Gaps) and w(x) = exp(-d_F(x)² / (2σ²)). Here d_F is the distance to the predicted occluding contour (the front face's landmark outline inside the back face's hull), with σ of about 4 to 8 px at 256-px scale. The watershed ridge then snaps to the strongest edge near the landmark-predicted contour instead of to any edge. Cost: about 1 ms in total (Scharr 0.13 ms, distanceTransform 0.3 ms, watershed 0.16 ms at 256 px; Local bench).
  3. Optional colour term: add the per-person LLR as a bias on R, or as a tie-breaker in a "contested" sub-band where edges are weak. Fit the GMMs only at contact onset or every 15 to 30 frames (35 to 45 ms per fit), and only score the band (about 1 ms per frame [unverified estimate, scoring only]).
  4. Exclusivity: produce a label map L ∈ {A, B, none}. Then alpha_A = 1[L=A] and alpha_B = 1[L=B], so alpha_A + alpha_B ≤ 1 everywhere. For anti-aliasing, feather only a 1 to 2 px band with a guided filter (1.6 ms at 256 px), then renormalise so alpha_A + alpha_B = alpha_union in the band.
- **When to escalate to graph cut / GrabCut.** Use it on contact frames where the watershed seam disagrees with the landmark prior by more than a threshold (for example a mean distance of more than 6 px), or where edge support along the seam is weak. Run one grabCut iteration on the band ROI only (24 ms at 256 px, so about 10 ms for a 96 x 256 band [unverified scaling estimate]). Use GC_FGD for A-certain, GC_BGD for B-certain and the outside, and GC_PR_FGD / GC_PR_BGD split by the landmark prior. OpenCV's grabCut does not accept custom unary terms, so the shape prior can only enter through the PR_FGD/PR_BGD initial labels. If custom unaries are needed, use a max-flow library on a 4-connected band graph [unverified: PyMaxflow runtime not measured here].
- **Random walker** gives a soft probability map (`return_full_prob=True`) that is a good input for temporal smoothing and for a confidence measure (pixels with P(A) near 0.5 are "unsure"), but it costs 27 ms at 256 px. Use it only on keyframes. For colour it needs per-channel normalisation (docstring note).
- **Geodesic splits** were the slowest option measured (117 to 520 ms). The marker-controlled watershed on a gradient relief gives a similar "the boundary follows the edge" behaviour at about 1/500 of the cost.
- **Edge-aware placement.** The occluding contour of the front face is normally a real image edge (jaw, cheek or nose silhouette against the other face, often with a thin shadow line). The back face's landmark outline inside the front face has no edge, because it is hallucinated. So edge evidence both places the seam and tells which face is in front (see section 3).

### Gaps
- No measured runtime for OpenCV structured edge detection (`cv2.ximgproc.createStructuredEdgeDetection`) in this session: the `model.yml.gz` download from github.com was blocked (403). Dollár and Zitnick's structured forests are commonly quoted as real-time [unverified]. A measurement on the target CPU is needed.

## 2. Learned instance separation on CPU (YOLO-seg, RTMDet-Ins, Mask R-CNN, multi-person parsing, promptable SAM variants, video propagation)

### Takeaway
No CPU-light learned model found here separates two faces in a kiss at seam quality. Person instance segmenters are whole-body, coarse at the face seam and degrade under heavy occlusion: Mask R-CNN scores about 0.16 to 0.17 AP on the OCHuman heavy-occlusion benchmark, and multi-person parsers lose accuracy quickly as overlap grows. Promptable SAM-family models are the best learned option: prompt with face A's landmarks as positives and face B's as negatives. On CPU they cost hundreds of ms to seconds per frame (encoder), so they belong to contact-onset keyframes only, with the result propagated by flow plus the cheap watershed seam. The strongest lesson from the literature is Pose2Seg: pose or keypoint priors separate tangled people better than boxes. The 106 landmarks are this pipeline's equivalent prior.

### Cited Findings
- Ultralytics YOLO26 segmentation (COCO, 640 px): YOLO26n-seg mAPbox 39.6, mAPmask 33.9, CPU ONNX 53.3 ± 0.5 ms, T4 TensorRT10 2.1 ms, 2.7M params, 9.3 GFLOPs. YOLO26s-seg mAPmask 40.0, CPU ONNX 118.4 ± 0.9 ms, 10.4M params. Speeds are "averaged over COCO val images using an Amazon EC2 P4d instance"; "CPU speeds measured with ONNX export". — [Ultralytics README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ultralytics/ultralytics/main/README.md) (docs.ultralytics.com was blocked; the segment docs page now lists YOLO26, not YOLO11)
- Pose2Seg (CVPR 2019) introduced OCHuman (8,110 human instances in 4,731 images with boxes, poses and masks), focused on heavily occluded people. Pose2Seg beats Mask R-CNN by about 50% AP there. A secondary review gives 0.222 vs 0.163 AP (val) and 0.238 vs 0.169 (test). With ground-truth keypoints, Pose2Seg reaches about 0.54 to 0.55 AP. The authors' argument is that pose skeletons separate tangled people better than boxes because they encode body-part locations and visibility. Secondary sources disagree slightly on the exact numbers; the CVPR tables are authoritative. — [Pose2Seg CVPR 2019](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Zhang_Pose2Seg_Detection_Free_Human_Instance_Segmentation_CVPR_2019_paper.html); [arXiv 1803.10683](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10683)
- Multi-human parsing degrades with overlap. A study on CIHP subsets grouped by overlap (RP R-CNN, AIParsing, M2FP, UniParser) found accuracy falls as overlap grows, and AIParsing "deteriorates more rapidly with increasing overlap". A CDGNet paper notes that "occlusion frequently occurs when multiple persons stand close together" on CIHP. SCHP is reported at 59.36% mIoU on CIHP, but no SCHP-specific close-contact failure analysis was found. — [Multi-view occlusion-aware multi-human parsing, arXiv 2509.10093](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.10093); [CDGNet arXiv 2111.14173](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.14173)
- EdgeSAM: 38.7 FPS on iPhone 14 (MobileSAM 4.9 FPS); 164.3 FPS on a 2080 Ti (MobileSAM 103.5, SAM 4.3). The speed benchmark runs encoder plus decoder with one prompt. ONNX files exist (`edge_sam_3x_encoder.onnx`, `edge_sam_3x_decoder.onnx`; tested with onnxruntime 1.16.3). The encoder takes a 1x3x1024x1024 input and outputs a 1x256x64x64 embedding. Point labels: 0 = negative, 1 = positive, 2/3 = box corners. COCO mask AP with ViTDet-H boxes: SAM 46.1, EdgeSAM 42.2, EdgeSAM-3x 42.7, MobileSAM 39.4. — [EdgeSAM README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chongzhou96/EdgeSAM/master/README.md)
- On a Qualcomm NPU (not CPU), fp16: EdgeSAM encoder 81 ms + decoder 11 ms = 92 ms for the first mask; MobileSAM encoder 320 ms (attributed to GELU) + decoder 11 ms. — [onnxsim PR #1876](https://github.com/onnxsim/onnxsim/pull/1876) (search summary; not independently checked)
- MobileSAM README: about 12 ms per image on GPU (8 ms encoder + 4 ms decoder). A rough laptop-CPU figure of about 3 s is mentioned, and it is unclear from the summary whether it refers to MobileSAM or SAM. — [MobileSAM GitHub](https://github.com/chaoningzhang/mobilesam) (search summary)
- SAM 2 on CPU (third-party): distilling SAM 2.1 Hiera-tiny reports 1956 ms/frame for the teacher (Hiera-tiny) and 920 ms for a MobileNetV4 lite version at 1024 input with 6 CPU threads, and 398 ms/frame for a 576-input mobile variant on 2 threads (laptop i7-13620H). — [sam2-lite on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/Pablopigue/sam2-lite) (search summary)
- EfficientTAM (efficient SAM 2-style video segmentation): about 10 FPS on an iPhone 15 Pro Max, and about 2x faster than SAM 2 on an A100 with comparable quality. No CPU latency was found. — [EfficientTAM arXiv 2411.18933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18933v1)
- Depth Anything V2 Small: 24.8M params, Apache-2.0 (Base/Large/Giant are CC-BY-NC-4.0), default input 518. No latency figures in the README. — [Depth-Anything-V2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2/main/README.md)

### Inferences
- **YOLO-seg / RTMDet-Ins / Mask R-CNN for a kiss.** These give person (whole-body) instances, not faces. At 640 input, the face contact zone covers a small part of the image and the mask head works at reduced resolution. Ultralytics' proto masks are at 1/4 input resolution [unverified recall], about 4 px granularity at 640 and worse after upscaling to the face. Instance masks are predicted independently and can overlap or both miss the contested pixels, so they still need an exclusivity step. The OCHuman numbers show box-driven instance segmentation is weak exactly when people are entangled. Verdict: at most a coarse "which person does this hair or shoulder belong to" cue. Not worth 53+ ms per frame (more on 2 vCPU [unverified]) for the face seam.
- **Promptable SAM recipe (keyframes only).** Crop a square contact ROI around both faces (for example 1.5x the union of the two face boxes) and resize it to the encoder's fixed 1024 px. The encoder cost is fixed, so a tight crop gives the most seam detail per ms. Prompt face A with 4 to 8 positive points (both eye centres, nose tip, mid-cheek points from the 106 set that lie in A-certain), plus 4 to 8 negatives from B-certain, and optionally A's landmark box (labels 2/3). Then do the reverse for B. One encoder run serves both faces; each decoder run is about 11 ms on the NPU figure above, so a few tens of ms on CPU [unverified]. Take the multimask output with the best IoU against hull ∩ XSeg. Then enforce exclusivity: in pixels both masks claim, use the order decision (section 3); in pixels neither claims, use watershed. The EdgeSAM encoder on 2 x86 vCPUs is estimated at a few hundred ms [unverified estimate from the 81 ms NPU fp16 number, the 2080 Ti FPS and SAM-family CPU reports], so it is affordable only at contact onset and every 10 to 30 frames during long contact.
- **Video propagation.** SAM 2 Hiera-tiny is about 2 s per frame on 6 CPU threads (sam2-lite report). XMem/Cutie CPU costs were not found but are assumed to be in the same order [unverified]. None of these fit a millisecond-class budget on 2 vCPU. The practical substitute is: keyframe mask (SAM or GrabCut) → DIS-flow warp (0.4 to 4.5 ms) → per-frame watershed re-snap with the warped labels as markers (about 1 ms) → periodic re-keying.
- **Pose2Seg lesson.** Keypoint-driven separation beat box-driven separation by about 50% AP on heavy occlusion. The compositor's 106 landmarks plus the exclusive outline rule already form a strong prior. The upgrade is to make the seam image-evidence-driven (edges, colour, keyframe SAM) within a few pixels of that prior, not to replace the prior.
- **BiSeNet face parsing** has no "other person" class, so the other person's cheek in A's crop is most likely labelled skin [unverified, not tested]. It still helps in two ways: hair, neck and background labels in A's crop mark non-A-face regions (for example B's hair across A's face), and nose and lip classes give near-certain markers for A.

### Gaps
- No direct CPU (x86, 2 thread) ONNX latency was found for EdgeSAM, MobileSAM, EfficientSAM, EfficientTAM, YOLO26n-seg, RTMDet-Ins-tiny or Depth Anything V2 Small. The model files could not be downloaded in this session (Hugging Face and GitHub binary downloads blocked), so they could not be benchmarked. Run onnxruntime on the target machine.
- No published evaluation was found of any learned model specifically on kissing or cheek-to-cheek face separation. Hi4D (close human interaction, CVPR 2023) may hold relevant instance masks [not checked].

- The YOLO11n-seg CPU figure (recalled as about 66 ms ONNX at 640) could not be confirmed; the current README lists YOLO26 only.

## 3. Front/behind order (occlusion-boundary ownership, landmark visibility, monocular depth, XSeg cross-checks, published ordering methods)

### Takeaway
The cheapest and most direct cue is boundary ownership. The front face's outline inside the overlap is a real image edge, while the back face's outline there is hallucinated and edgeless. Measure mean edge strength along each face's outline segment that lies inside the other face's hull, and the face with the stronger edge is in front. Combine this with an XSeg cross-check, a colour-LLR test on the overlap pixels and landmark jitter, and decide with hysteresis. Learned pairwise ordering networks exist (InstaOrderNet: image plus two instance masks → occlusion order and depth order). Monocular depth (Depth Anything V2 Small) is a weak, expensive tie-breaker at the few-cm depth differences of a kiss.

### Cited Findings
- InstaOrder (Lee and Park, CVPR 2022): about 101K natural scenes with about 2.9M instance-wise orderings, where occlusion order and depth order are labelled for the same instances. The authors report that "the occlusion order and depth order are complementary". InstaOrderNet (variants ^o, ^d, ^{o,d}) recovers the orders from a single image and beats PCNet-M and OrderNet^{M+I} on COCOA, KINS and InstaOrder. InstaDepthNet adds an auxiliary geometric-order loss on top of MiDaS. COCOA was the first amodal dataset with modal and amodal masks plus pairwise occlusion orders, but has only 5,073 images. Code and data: POSTECH-CVLab/InstaOrder. — [InstaOrder arXiv 2111.14562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14562); [CVPR 2022 open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/html/Lee_Instance-Wise_Occlusion_and_Depth_Orders_in_Natural_Scenes_CVPR_2022_paper.html)
- Occlusion-boundary ownership networks: DOOBNet predicts boundaries and their occlusion orientation (an angle per edge pixel, foreground on the "left" by convention) in one pass. DOC (Wang and Yuille) is the earlier single-image boundary-ownership network. Pre-deep-learning pipelines segmented first and then labelled figure/ground per boundary fragment. P2ORM encodes, per pixel pair, whether one pixel occludes its neighbour. — [DOOBNet arXiv 1806.03772](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.03772); [P2ORM arXiv 2007.12088](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.12088.pdf)
- Pose2Seg relies on pose skeletons that encode body-part location and visibility to separate occluded people, which supports using per-landmark visibility as an ordering and separation cue. — [Pose2Seg CVPR 2019](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Zhang_Pose2Seg_Detection_Free_Human_Instance_Segmentation_CVPR_2019_paper.html)
- Depth Anything V2 Small: 24.8M params, 518 default input, Apache-2.0. Reported data points (not x86 CPU): about 17 ms at 256x256 with ONNX on a Snapdragon X Elite (user report), and 31.1 ms fp16 on an iPhone 12 Pro Max (Core ML, mainly Neural Engine). — [Depth-Anything-V2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2/main/README.md); [Qualcomm HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/qualcomm/Depth-Anything-V2/discussions/1); [Apple Core ML model card](https://huggingface.co/apple/coreml-depth-anything-v2-small) (search summaries)
- FaceFusion's occlusion path takes the per-pixel minimum across XSeg models (`numpy.minimum.reduce(temp_masks)`) when several are selected ('many'), which is the "most-occluded wins" rule. — [FaceFusion face_masker.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facefusion/facefusion/master/facefusion/face_masker.py)

### Inferences
- **Order evidence, ranked by cost and value (all [unverified] as a combined method; each is a design proposal built on the findings above).** O = hullA ∩ hullB in frame coordinates. Skip the order decision when area(O) < about 1% of the smaller face.
  1. *Boundary-ownership edge score* (about 0.5 ms). Sample the Scharr or structured-edge magnitude along A's jaw/cheek contour points (106-pt contour indices) that fall inside hullB: E_A. Do the same for B's contour inside hullA: E_B. Normalise each by the same face's mean edge strength on its free (non-overlapping) contour. The front face has the real contour, so score s1 = log(E_A / E_B) > 0 means A is in front. Optionally check the T-junction geometry: where the two outlines cross, the front contour continues through the junction, while the back contour ends at it.
  2. *XSeg cross-check* (no extra inference; XSeg already runs per face). Map each face's XSeg into frame coordinates. If A is in front, XSeg_A should mark O as visible face while XSeg_B should mark O as occluded, if xseg_2 treats another face as an occluder (see Gaps). s2 = mean(XSeg_A on O) − mean(XSeg_B on O).
  3. *Colour LLR on O* (about 1 ms once the GMMs are fitted). O's pixels belong to the front person. s3 = mean over O of log p_A(x) − log p_B(x). Trust it only when |s3| is large (distinct skin, beard, make-up).
  4. *Landmark stability* (free). The back face's landmarks inside O are guessed, so their frame-to-frame jitter, relative to that face's own stable points, should be higher. s4 = jitter_B(O) − jitter_A(O).
  5. *Monocular depth* (contact onset only). Run Depth Anything V2 Small on the contact ROI at a low input such as 256 to 364 px [unverified CPU cost; likely tens to a few hundred ms on 2 vCPU] and compare median relative inverse depth on A-certain vs B-certain. In a kiss the noses and cheeks are a few cm apart, so the depth difference is near the model's local noise; use it only as a tie-breaker.
  6. *Learned pairwise order* (optional, offline-heavy). Run InstaOrderNet on (ROI image, mask A, mask B) at contact onset; it is trained on COCO-style scenes that include people. CPU cost was not found.
- **Decision rule.** Combine S = Σ w_i · clip(s_i) over the available cues. Suggested starting weights [unverified]: edge 0.4, XSeg 0.25, colour 0.15, jitter 0.1, depth 0.1. Commit to an order only when |S| exceeds a threshold for N consecutive frames (for example 5 to 8 frames at 25 to 30 fps). Keep the previous order otherwise. Re-evaluate only when the contact geometry changes substantially (O area changes by more than 30% or contact starts or ends).
- **Mutual occlusion.** In a kiss each face can be in front in a different sub-region (A's nose over B's cheek, B's lips over A's chin). A single global order fails there. Evaluate the edge-ownership score per contour segment (for example in 4 to 6 segments along the overlap) and let the seam's owner switch along its length. InstaOrder explicitly models complementary occlusion and depth orders, which supports treating order as not always a single global relation [inference].

### Gaps
- InstaOrderNet accuracy numbers, input resolution, backbone and CPU cost could not be read (ar5iv was blocked; only abstract-level summaries were available).
- No published method was found that orders two overlapping faces specifically, or that evaluates boundary ownership on face-to-face contact.
- No x86 CPU latency for Depth Anything V2 Small or MiDaS small was found. The MiDaS small runtime is not covered here.
- The 106-point landmarker (InsightFace 2d106det-style) gives no per-point visibility or confidence output [unverified]. Jitter is a proxy.

## 4. Temporal stability of the separation boundary

### Takeaway
Propagate the previous frame's label map with DIS optical flow (0.36 ms ULTRAFAST to 3.8 ms MEDIUM at 256 px on 2 cores), use the warped labels, eroded, as watershed markers, and re-snap the seam to the current frame's edges each frame (about 1 ms). Smooth the seam as a signed distance field, not as a mask, and apply hysteresis both to the seam position (a dead band of about 1 to 2 px) and to the front/behind order (N-frame confirmation). Refresh with an expensive keyframe solver (GrabCut, random walker or SAM) only at contact onset or on drift.

### Cited Findings
- Measured DIS optical flow on 2 cores: ULTRAFAST 0.36 / 1.01 / 2.20 ms, FAST 1.23 / 2.72 / 4.54 ms, MEDIUM 3.76 / 8.17 / 13.30 ms at 256 / 384 / 512 px. Farneback took 10.4 / 27.8 / 49.0 ms. — Local bench
- SAM 2-style memory propagation costs about 1 to 2 s per frame on CPU even for Hiera-tiny variants (sam2-lite report: 1956 ms teacher and 920 ms lite at 1024 on 6 threads; 398 ms for the 576 variant on 2 threads). EfficientTAM runs at about 10 FPS on an iPhone 15 Pro Max. — [sam2-lite](https://huggingface.co/Pablopigue/sam2-lite); [EfficientTAM arXiv 2411.18933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18933v1)
- Deep-Live-Cam keeps its blend mask geometry-based and cached ("identical every frame for the same model input size, so it contributes no jitter"), which shows the general design choice of preferring geometry-locked masks for temporal stability. — Deep-Live-Cam `modules/processors/frame/face_swapper.py`, `_create_elliptical_mask` docstring ([Deep-Live-Cam repository](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam); inspected from a local copy of the file in this session)

### Inferences
- **Per-frame loop (about 3 to 6 ms total at 256 px ROI, 2 cores; [unverified sum of the measured parts]).**
  1. DIS flow (FAST, 1.2 ms) from frame t−1 to t on the contact ROI, in grey.
  2. Warp L_{t−1} (labels A/B/none) with the flow. Erode each warped label by 3 to 4 px to form markers. Pixels within ±3 px of the warped seam are left unlabelled.
  3. Intersect the markers with this frame's landmark-certain regions: landmark evidence overrides the flow where they conflict. This prevents flow drift from accumulating.
  4. Watershed on the edge × prior relief (section 1) gives the new seam (0.2 ms).
  5. Temporal filter: convert the A/B seam to a signed distance field φ_t (two distanceTransforms, 0.6 ms). Then φ̂_t = φ_t if |φ_t − warp(φ̂_{t−1})| > δ (a real move, δ about 2 px), otherwise α·φ_t + (1−α)·warp(φ̂_{t−1}) with α about 0.3 to 0.5. The final owner is sign(φ̂_t). This is the dead band, and it stops one-pixel flicker without lagging real motion.
  6. Feather with the guided filter or the 0.5-iso remap (1.6 ms) and renormalise the alphas.
- **Drift and refresh triggers.** Re-key with GrabCut (1 iteration, band ROI) or SAM when (a) the seam is more than 6 to 8 px from the landmark prior over more than 20% of its length, (b) edge support along the seam falls below a threshold, (c) contact starts (first frame with area(O) above threshold) or (d) a shot cut is detected.
- **Order hysteresis.** Commit to a front/behind change only after 5 to 8 consecutive frames of a confident score (section 3). Then blend the change over 3 to 5 frames through the signed distance field rather than flipping ownership in one frame.
- **Contact onset and release.** Ramp the exclusive cut-out in and out over a few frames as area(O) crosses its threshold, so the seam does not pop when the faces first touch.

### Gaps
- No published method was found for temporal smoothing of a two-face contact seam specifically. The loop above is a design built from measured components, not a tested system.
- XMem and Cutie CPU costs were not found.
- The quality of DIS flow on low-texture skin at the contact (where flow is least reliable) was not measured. Landmark-driven markers are the intended safeguard.

## 5. What face-swap tools do when two faces overlap

### Takeaway
None of the inspected tools separates two touching faces. FaceFusion's masks (box, occlusion via XSeg, area via landmark hull, region via BiSeNet) are computed per face crop and have no notion of a second face. Deep-Live-Cam's "many faces" mode swaps faces one after another onto the same frame, so at an overlap the face processed last wins, whatever the true depth order. DeepFaceLab's practice is manual: XSeg is trained on hand-labelled masks where users exclude obstructions; nothing found documents how a second face is handled. The compositor's exclusive-ownership rule is already beyond what these tools do.

### Cited Findings
- FaceFusion `face_masker.py` has four mask types: box (padded, blurred rectangle, no model), occlusion (XSeg models `xseg_1`, `xseg_2`, `xseg_3`, 256x256, from DeepFaceLab, GPL-3.0; 'many' runs all three and takes the per-pixel minimum), area (convex hull of 68 landmarks selected by `face_mask_areas`) and region (BiSeNet `bisenet_resnet_18` / `_34`, 512x512, MIT, union of selected classes via `numpy.isin`). "There is no handling" of multiple faces: every function takes one crop and one set of landmarks, and there is no face loop or logic to tell the target face from other faces. — [FaceFusion face_masker.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facefusion/facefusion/master/facefusion/face_masker.py)
- FaceFusion face selection is identity-based: in 'reference' mode, target faces are matched to a reference face by embedding distance `1 - dot(embedding_norm_a, embedding_norm_b)`, remapped from [0, 2] to [0, 1] and compared to `reference_face_distance`. 'many' mode processes all faces. — FaceFusion `facefusion/face_selector.py` ([FaceFusion repository](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion); inspected from a local copy in this session)
- FaceFusion's default occluder setting is `--face-occluder-model` default `xseg_1`, with choices from the `FaceOccluderModel` type. — FaceFusion `facefusion/program.py` ([FaceFusion repository](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion); local copy)
- Deep-Live-Cam with `many_faces`: `for face in many_faces: current_swap_target = swap_face(source_face, face, current_swap_target)`, so each swap is pasted onto the result of the previous one, in detection order. Its blend masks are a fixed ellipse (axes 0.44 x crop size, heavily blurred) in aligned space and an optional lower-mouth mask. There is no inter-face occlusion mask. — Deep-Live-Cam `modules/processors/frame/face_swapper.py` ([Deep-Live-Cam repository](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam); inspected from a local copy in this session)
- DeepFaceLab: XSeg mask editing is a core step (label, train, apply, re-edit). Users mask objects in front of the face so they are not swapped (issue #686). No guidance on a second face was found in the results. The DeepFaceLab paper claims occlusion handling in general terms. — [DeepFaceLab issue #686](https://github.com/iperov/DeepFaceLab/issues/686); [DeepFaceLab paper arXiv 2005.05535](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.05535.pdf)

### Inferences
- In DFL practice, the usual way to handle a second face is to label it as outside the target's XSeg polygon (an exclude region) on enough kissing frames, so a custom-trained XSeg learns the contact seam per scene [unverified; reported in a search summary as general knowledge, not confirmed by a primary source]. That is per-video manual training, which the compositor wants to avoid. It also suggests why a generic xseg_2 may or may not cut the other face out: it depends on whether its training labels did so.
- Deep-Live-Cam's sequential paste means the swap order (detection order) silently decides who is in front. The compositor's explicit ownership rule plus an order decision avoids that failure.
- Because no reference tool offers this, the recommended pipeline (landmark-prior markers → edge-snapped watershed seam → exclusive alphas → flow plus SDF hysteresis → keyframe GrabCut or SAM, plus an edge-ownership order score) would be new ground for this project and must be validated on the project's own kiss and cheek-to-cheek clips.

### Gaps
- DeepFaceLive's handling of overlapping faces was not inspected (no source retrieved). It is assumed, not verified, to work per face like DeepFaceLab.
- No official DeepFaceLab documentation on labelling a second face in XSeg was found (the repository is archived; guides are mostly on community forums).
