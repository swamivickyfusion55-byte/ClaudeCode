# Lock identity before contact, snap seams to edges

The most reliable way to keep two swapped faces straight through a kiss, a cheek-to-cheek pose or a hug is to treat contact as an evidence blackout. Settle identity from clean anchors before the faces touch. Re-confirm it after they separate with several frames of summed evidence. Place the seam between the faces on real image edges within a few pixels of the landmark outline. Take geometry from the track, never from the raw detector. ArcFace forces this design. A crop that mixes two faces behaves like a face morph, and in one study **91.3% of morphs verified against both contributing identities** at a 1% false-match rate ([arXiv 2305.18216](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18216)). Plain occlusion, by contrast, lowers genuine scores and leaves impostor scores where they were ([Damer et al.](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/bme2.12044)). A kiss-frame embedding therefore cannot justify a relabel in either direction. Soft cues help only at the margins. Within one person, measured skin lightness varies across images by about **three times** the average true difference between Black and White subject groups ([Howard et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11240)), and per-frame gender classifiers have large error gaps between groups. Both must enter as clipped, per-track terms learned on non-contact frames, never as filters. For the pixels, a marker-controlled watershed on an edge map weighted by the landmark prior costs about **1 ms [measured]** at 256 px. One GrabCut iteration costs 24 ms and a geodesic split 117 ms. Colour models serve only as tie-breakers, because similar skin defeats them. The largest remaining disfigurement risk is geometric, not generative. SCRFD's hard NMS at IoU 0.4 can delete the second face. A merged box centres the 106-landmark crop between two heads. A 5-px error on one hidden mouth corner changes the inswapper crop scale by about 4.6%. FaceFusion, Deep-Live-Cam, Rope and roop all match each detection on its own against a single cosine threshold; FaceFusion's default equals cosine > 0.4, and it adds hard gender, race and age filters. None of them enforces one source per person. The engine already goes beyond these tools on ownership, crossing locks and carry. The ranked additions below are mostly millisecond-class: box hygiene, rigid-point alignment, sequential re-binding, template memory and edge-snapped seams. Evidence labels: **[measured]** means timed either on the research bench (Intel Xeon 2.1 GHz pinned to 2 cores, OpenCV-contrib 4.10, scikit-image 0.26) or in the engine's own production profile. **[sourced]** means stated in a cited source or read from code. **[unverified]** means an estimate or design proposal not validated in this research.

## Kiss-frame embeddings match both people, so identity is settled outside contact

The recogniser is strong on clean pairs. The buffalo_l pack (SCRFD-10GF, w600k_r50) reports **99.33 on CFP-FP** (frontal versus profile) and **97.25 TAR at FAR 1e-4 on IJB-C** ([InsightFace model zoo](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface/blob/master/model_zoo/README.md)). Its weakness in contact is mechanical. The ArcFace wrapper builds its 112-px crop from a similarity transform on the **5 SCRFD keypoints alone** ([arcface_onnx.py](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface/blob/master/python-package/insightface/model_zoo/arcface_onnx.py)). If an eye or mouth keypoint lands on the partner during a kiss, the crop contains the partner. Occlusion literature then separates two very different cases. NIST found that masks pushed the best algorithms' false non-match rate from about 0.3% to about 5%, while false positives "stayed stable or fell slightly" ([NIST IR 8311](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2020/NIST.IR.8311.pdf)). Damer et al. likewise found that, for ArcFace-class systems, occlusion shifts the genuine distribution toward the impostor one and leaves the impostor distribution largely unchanged ([IET Biometrics 2021](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/bme2.12044)). A face half hidden behind its partner is therefore safe: it scores low against its own slot, not high against the other. The dangerous case is the **mixed crop**, cheek, nose and mouth of both people in one aligned image, which acts like a crude morph and can clear the threshold against both slots ([arXiv 2305.18216](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18216)). Pose and quality move scores too. ArcFace accuracy dropped from 95.6% to about 93.8% beyond 30° of head rotation in one forensic evaluation ([arXiv 2311.06145](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.06145)). In a screening study, filtering low-quality inputs raised the mean genuine cosine from **0.66 to 0.76** ([arXiv 2507.15961](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15961v1)). No published curve of ArcFace cosine against occluded-area fraction exists, and no study measures natural two-face crops. Morphing is the closest proxy and it is imperfect, since morphs are pixel blends of aligned faces, not partial overlaps.

No official same-person threshold exists for w600k_r50. Practitioner defaults range from 0.25 to 0.5. BoT-FaceSORT, the one face-specific tracker in the notes, accepts an appearance match at **cosine ≥ 0.4** ([botfacesort.yaml](https://github.com/bellhyeon/BoT-FaceSORT/blob/master/tracker/configs/botfacesort.yaml)). FaceFusion's default is equivalent to the same value (see the last section). ArcFace thresholds on IJB-B span 0.328 to 0.823 as the false-accept rate moves from 1e-1 to 1e-3 ([AdvHat, arXiv 1908.08705](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.08705)), so any absolute number must be calibrated on the project's own footage. The engine's 0.38/0.40 walls and its re-entry steal at 0.40 sit at the same operating point as FaceFusion and BoT-FaceSORT, which is reassuring. For two people, though, the absolute value matters less than the **gap** `s(A,d) − s(B,d)`. Two different actors sit near zero against each other on clean frames (the engine's own tuning notes record this; local: `swap_engine.py`), and mixed crops compress exactly that gap. Logging the gap per frame is the single most useful diagnostic for kiss clips.

### Template memory must be frozen at contact and kept as a bank

Every mainstream tracker smooths its appearance template with an exponential moving average at momentum 0.9: BoT-SORT hard-codes it ([bot_sort.py](https://github.com/NirAharon/BoT-SORT/blob/main/tracker/bot_sort.py)), and StrongSORT defaults to it ([opts.py](https://github.com/dyhBUPT/StrongSORT/blob/master/opts.py)). At that momentum, ten consecutive mixed crops already hold **65% of the template's weight** (1 − 0.9¹⁰), which is why freezing updates during contact, as the engine does, is essential rather than optional. The better trackers make updates confidence-dependent. Deep OC-SORT sets the momentum to `0.95 + 0.05·(1 − trust)`, where trust rescales the detection score above its threshold, so a detection at threshold changes nothing ([Deep-OC-SORT ocsort.py](https://github.com/GerardMaggiolino/Deep-OC-SORT/blob/main/trackers/integrated_ocsort_embedding/ocsort.py)). Hybrid-SORT weights by relative confidence and keeps a 30-entry long-term bank ([hybrid_sort_reid.py](https://github.com/ymzis69/HybridSORT/blob/master/trackers/hybrid_sort_tracker/hybrid_sort_reid.py)). BoT-FaceSORT stores every smoothed template per track and scores a detection by its **minimum distance to any stored template** ([bot_facesort.py](https://github.com/bellhyeon/BoT-FaceSORT/blob/master/tracker/trackers/botfacesort/bot_facesort.py)). For this pipeline that suggests a four-part identity per slot **[unverified design, sourced mechanisms]**. The first part is the locked user reference R, which the engine already holds. The second is a rolling template updated only through a confidence-scaled EMA. The third is a snapshot T_pre frozen at contact entry. The fourth is a bank of the 10–30 best-quality past embeddings. The slot's appearance score is the maximum similarity over all four. Taking the maximum over a bank tolerates pose change, because the best-matching stored pose wins.

Admission to the bank needs a quality gate, and the cheapest signal is free. InsightFace exposes the raw `embedding_norm` on every face ([common.py](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface/blob/master/python-package/insightface/app/common.py)). MagFace treats embedding magnitude as quality ([MagFace](https://github.com/IrvingMeng/MagFace)). AdaFace shows that the feature norm correlates with image quality for ArcFace-margin training as well ([arXiv 2204.00964](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.00964)). A practical gate combines several tests. Detector score should be at least 0.6–0.7, in line with StrongSORT's 0.6 and BoT-SORT's 0.7 new-track floor. Yaw should stay within about 30–35°. Inter-ocular distance should be above about 30–40 px. The raw norm should be above a clip-relative percentile. The hard rule on top is "not in contact". The numeric gates are **[unverified]** and need calibration. Whether w600k_r50's norm is a good quality proxy is supported only indirectly.

### Generic trackers let overlap decide, which is backwards in a kiss

The association rules in popular trackers become hazards when two faces overlap. BoT-SORT and BoT-FaceSORT fuse costs as `min(iou_dist, emb_dist)` ([bot_sort.py](https://github.com/NirAharon/BoT-SORT/blob/main/tracker/bot_sort.py)), so a large IoU can override contrary appearance. Deep OC-SORT skips appearance entirely whenever the IoU matrix is already one-to-one ([association.py](https://github.com/GerardMaggiolino/Deep-OC-SORT/blob/main/trackers/integrated_ocsort_embedding/association.py)). In a kiss, track A's predicted box overlaps B's detection, so both shortcuts can lock in a crossing swap. Deep OC-SORT's adaptive weighting points the right way but stops short. It raises the appearance weight from 0.75 toward 1.25 only when the top-1 versus top-2 cosine gap is large ([association.py](https://github.com/GerardMaggiolino/Deep-OC-SORT/blob/main/trackers/integrated_ocsort_embedding/association.py)). In contact that gap collapses, yet appearance keeps its 0.75 base weight instead of being switched off. No tracker in the notes publishes an explicit crossing lock, so the engine's rule (embeddings frozen and relabels blocked while two tracks overlap) has no off-the-shelf equivalent and should stay. When the detector returns one merged box or misses one face, the safest move is this. Assign the single detection to the slot with a clear appearance margin, or else by last observation. Mark the frame "low confidence, no template update". Hold the other slot in a hidden state. Both slots can live indefinitely, because the user defined them; the tracker never needs to create a new identity during contact.

### Separation should be decided by summed evidence over several clean frames

Re-identification after contact is the moment identity is actually at risk, and the trackers supply three ingredients for it. The first is appearance against **pre-occlusion** templates (BoT-FaceSORT's memory). The second is motion continuity. OC-SORT's direction-consistency term estimates heading from 3 frames back with weight 0.2, and its OCR stage re-matches lost tracks against their **last observation** rather than a drifted Kalman prediction ([OC-SORT, arXiv 2203.14360](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.14360); [args.py](https://github.com/noahcao/OC_SORT/blob/master/utils/args.py)). The third is ORU, which repairs the trajectory once the track is found again. A two-slot protocol built on these is straightforward **[unverified design]**. Declare separation only when the boxes have IoU 0 and centres more than about one face width apart for 2–3 frames, with both detections passing the quality gate. Then compute, per frame, the hypothesis evidence `Δ = [s(A,d1) + s(B,d2)] − [s(A,d2) + s(B,d1)]`, with each `s` taken as the maximum over R, T_pre and the bank. Add a motion term that favours each slot's pre-contact side and heading. Keep the pre-contact binding by default. Switch only when the **running sum** of Δ favours the crossed hypothesis by more than a margin of about 0.10–0.15 (summed cosine) for about 5 frames at 25–30 fps. Then relabel the buffered frames retroactively, which the chunked pipeline allows. The engine's existing flip rule (margin 0.14 sustained over 5 frames; local: `swap_engine.py`) already sits in this range. The additions are scoring Δ against T_pre and the bank rather than one lock, summing it over the window rather than testing frame by frame, and passing every relabel path, including the 0.40 re-entry steal and the un-cross check, through the same post-separation window. That way, the first motion-blurred, still-mixed frame after a kiss cannot trigger a steal. After a shot cut, BoT-FaceSORT switches to appearance-only association, with cuts detected by a colour-histogram difference above 0.4 ([bot_facesort.py](https://github.com/bellhyeon/BoT-FaceSORT/blob/master/tracker/trackers/botfacesort/bot_facesort.py)). That is the right reset for motion and side priors.

## Skin, gender, age, hair and clothing earn a vote only after they prove separable

Attribute cues have an awkward profile. They are most useful exactly where embeddings fail (profiles, contact), and most corrupted on those same frames. The resolution is to **learn each person's attribute model on clean, non-contact frames**, evaluate per-frame likelihoods only on regions known to be unoccluded, and give each cue zero weight unless the two people differ by clearly more than the cue's own noise. The tracking literature supplies the fusion pattern: gate, weight, then assign jointly. DeepSORT gates on Kalman Mahalanobis distance at χ² = 9.4877 (95%, 4 degrees of freedom), sets gated pairs to an effectively infinite cost and solves the assignment globally ([linear_assignment.py](https://github.com/nwojke/deep_sort/blob/master/deep_sort/linear_assignment.py)). For two slots, the proposed cost per track–detection pair is `w_emb·(1 − cos) + w_mot·min(D²/9.49, 3) + Σ_k w_k·clip(−log P_k, 0, c_k)` over the attribute cues k **[unverified design]**. Here `w_k = 0` unless the A-versus-B separation of cue k exceeds three times its within-track spread on the current shot, and each term is clipped at 1–2 nats, so no attribute can override agreeing embedding and motion. The decision is a log-likelihood ratio between the two permutations, with the same margin-and-hysteresis rule as above. Hard vetoes are reserved for physical certainties: one detection cannot belong to two people, and motion cannot jump beyond the gate.

| Cue | Reliable when | Set weight to zero when | Per-frame cost | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Skin colour (h*, L* from far cheek and forehead) | Same shot, distinct tones, patch outside the partner's box | Tones within 3× within-track spread; across cuts | < 1 ms per face | Variance figures **[sourced]**; cost **[unverified]** |
| Gender (genderage, log-odds per track) | Both tracks confidently labelled, labels differ | Same-gender couple, either track uncertain, contact frames | 0.6 ms per face, already run | **[measured, production profile]** |
| Age | Per-track medians differ by more than about 20 years | Adults of similar age | Same model as gender | MAE figures **[sourced]**; 20-year rule **[unverified]** |
| Hair and torso colour histograms | Before and after contact, within one shot | Torso boxes overlap (IoU > 0.1); models closer than about 0.3 Bhattacharyya | < 1 ms | **[unverified]** |
| Race labels | Never | Always | n/a | Group-dependent error **[sourced]** |

### Skin tone is a shot-local tie-breaker, not an identity

Skin colour from uncontrolled images is far noisier than intuition suggests. Howard et al. found that one subject's face-area lightness ranged by **38–39 units** from image to image. That is roughly three times the average true lightness difference between the Black and White subject groups ([arXiv 2106.11240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11240)). The individual typology angle (ITA) is sensitive to brightness and to "lack of yellow chroma", and is only meaningful on masked skin ([Kalb et al., arXiv 2308.09640](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.09640)). Thong et al. report that the **hue angle h\*** is robust to face relighting, making it a better companion to L* than ITA ([Beyond Skin Tone, ICCV 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05148)). These were cross-image studies, not consecutive video frames. Within one shot the variance should be smaller, but no source measures it. The practical recipe **[unverified]** is as follows. Convert to CIE-Lab. Sample the far-side cheek and the forehead, in patches about 0.15 of the inter-ocular distance, away from eyes, brows, lips and hairline. Reject pixels with L* below 15 or above 95 or more than 2.5 MAD from the patch median. Drop any patch that falls inside the partner's box, because the touching cheek is shadowed, tinted by inter-reflection, or partly the partner's skin. Keep per-track running medians of L* and h*. Use the cue only when the medians differ by more than three times the larger within-track spread on that shot. Re-learn after every cut. Couples of similar tone are common, so the fusion must degrade to "no information", not force a decision.

### Gender and age need per-track accumulation, and the slot should learn them from the target

The InsightFace genderage model is tiny (about 1.3 MB). It also takes a crop **centred on the bounding box at 1.5× its larger side with rotation fixed at 0**, with no landmark alignment ([attribute.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/deepinsight/insightface/master/python-package/insightface/model_zoo/attribute.py)). In a kiss that crop contains a large part of the partner, and tilted heads stay tilted. No official accuracy exists for it. A third-party table lists InsightFace models at about **84.7–87.5% gender accuracy** ([Savchenko, arXiv 2103.17107](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.17107)), but which file that row corresponds to is unconfirmed. FairFace-trained classifiers reach roughly 94–96% gender accuracy with under 1% gap between groups on curated faces ([FairFace](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.04913)). FaceFusion ships one as `fairface.onnx`, which uses a landmark-aligned 224-px crop ([face_classifier.py](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/3.9.2/facefusion/face_classifier.py)). MiVOLO is stronger (97–99% gender), but its authors call ONNX export "not advisable" ([MiVOLO](https://github.com/WildChlamydia/MiVOLO)). Bias is the reason gender must never veto. Gender Shades found worst-case errors of **34.7% for darker-skinned women against 0.8% for lighter-skinned men** across commercial systems of 2018 ([PMLR v81](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v81/buolamwini18a.html)). A 2025 audit still measured a Black-female true-positive rate of only 0.650 for a FairFace-trained model ([arXiv 2510.17873](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17873)). Video aggregation is the documented remedy: Demirkus et al. carried a Bayesian posterior through frames and reached about 90% by the final frame in unconstrained video ([CVPRW 2010](https://mlanthology.org/cvprw/2010/demirkus2010cvprw-gender)).

For this pipeline **[unverified design]**, accumulate per track a clipped log-odds `Σ w_t·clip(logit p_female, −2, 2)`, with weight equal to detector score × frontalness × no-overlap. Declare a track's gender only when the weighted mean exceeds 1.0 (mean probability ≥ 0.73) over at least 10 good frames. Let it vote only when both tracks are declared and differ. The model already runs at **0.6 ms per face [measured, production profile]** (local: `core_pipeline.py`), so this costs nothing new. Age is weaker still. Human annotators err by 7.22 years on average ([MiVOLO paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04616)), so age is worth a vote only when the two medians differ by decades, as with an adult and a child. Race labels should not be used at all. They duplicate the weaker skin cue and carry group-dependent error.

One local observation follows directly. The engine's late-join path takes each slot's gender from the **replacement photo** and steers unreferenced faces toward the slot of matching gender (local: `core_pipeline.py`, `swap_engine.py`). That works only when the replacement and the target person share a gender. A cross-gender swap would steer the target toward the wrong slot. The research supports learning the gender prior from the target person's own clean frames instead.

### Hair and clothing separate a couple before and after a hug, not during it

Context beyond the face is a well-established identity cue. In the PIPA photo-album benchmark only about **52% of person instances had a usable frontal face**, which is why the PIPER recogniser combined body, poselet and face cues ([Zhang et al., arXiv 1501.05703](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.05703)). Gallagher and Chen found that clothing segmented below the face "noticeably improves recognition accuracy" ([CVPR 2008](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/2008/gallagher2008cvpr-clothing)). TV-series identification fuses face and person tracks because bodies stay recognisable when faces are occluded ([BMVC 2015](https://bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/2015/papers/paper119/abstract119.pdf)). Two building blocks suit a CPU budget. One is an HSV histogram with hue and saturation binned jointly and value kept separate, so shading changes touch only the V part ([arXiv 1612.06454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.06454)). The other is the Bhattacharyya distance between a track's stored histogram and the current one ([arXiv 1511.01726](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.01726)). A torso box scaled to the face is a reasonable starting geometry **[unverified]**: about one face width either side of the centre, from half a face height to two and a half face heights below the chin. A hair band about 0.3–0.6 face heights above the brows, minus skin, completes it. The cue's limits in contact are physical. Torsos interpenetrate, arms cross, the front person covers the other's clothes, and couples often wear similar black or white. So switch the cue off whenever the torso boxes overlap at IoU above about 0.1. Learn each model only from unobstructed frames. Zero its weight when the two models are closer than about 0.3 Bhattacharyya. Treat it as within-shot only, because clothing does not survive a scene change. Used this way it supplies independent evidence at the exact moment that matters, the first clean frames after separation, for well under a millisecond.

## Edges, not colour, should place the seam between two touching faces

The engine already enforces one face per pixel at full strength and decides front and behind with an outline-edge vote. What remains open is **where** the seam runs inside the overlap, and what happens when each face is in front in a different place. No reference tool helps here. FaceFusion computes every mask from one face's own crop and landmarks, with no notion of a second face ([face_masker.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facefusion/facefusion/master/facefusion/face_masker.py)). Deep-Live-Cam pastes faces one after another, so the face processed last wins whatever the true depth order ([Deep-Live-Cam](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam)). DeepFaceLab relies on hand-labelled XSeg masks per project ([DFL issue #686](https://github.com/iperov/DeepFaceLab/issues/686)). The closest research lesson is Pose2Seg. On the OCHuman heavy-occlusion benchmark, pose-driven instance segmentation beat box-driven Mask R-CNN by about 50% (about 0.22 against 0.16 AP), and reached about 0.54 AP with ground-truth keypoints ([Pose2Seg, CVPR 2019](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Zhang_Pose2Seg_Detection_Free_Human_Instance_Segmentation_CVPR_2019_paper.html)). Keypoints separate tangled people better than boxes. The 106 landmarks are this pipeline's keypoints, so the upgrade is to let image evidence move the seam a few pixels off the landmark prior, not to replace the prior.

### A watershed on an edge-times-prior relief costs about a millisecond

The research bench timed the classical options at contact-ROI sizes on 2 cores **[measured]** (local: `research_notes/Face swap contact identity binding/pixel_separation_of_touching_faces.md`). The spread runs from sub-millisecond to half a second, and it decides what can run on every contact frame.

| Operation (2 cores) | 256 px | 512 px | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cv2.watershed`, 3 markers | 0.16 ms | 1.15 ms | Per-frame seam |
| Scharr gradient magnitude | 0.13 ms | 0.54 ms | Edge relief and ownership score |
| Two `cv2.distanceTransform` calls | 0.57 ms | 2.48 ms | Prior weighting, signed distance |
| `cv2.ximgproc.guidedFilter`, r = 4 | 1.64 ms | 7.03 ms | 1–2 px feather |
| DIS flow, ULTRAFAST / FAST | 0.36 / 1.23 ms | 2.20 / 4.54 ms | Carry labels between frames |
| Per-person Lab GMM fit (3 components, ×2) | 36–40 ms | 37 ms | Contact onset only |
| `cv2.grabCut`, 1 / 3 iterations | 24.1 / 47.1 ms | 107.5 / 223.5 ms | Escalation only |
| `random_walker` (Lab, cg_j) | 27.2 ms | 118.2 ms | Keyframe soft probabilities |
| Geodesic split (2× MCP_Geometric) | 117 ms | 520 ms | Not worth it |

The recommended per-frame seam **[unverified design, measured components]** works in a contact ROI. Take the overlap of the two dilated landmark hulls plus a margin, resampled to about 192–256 px. Seed three markers. "A-certain" is A's hull intersected with A's XSeg, eroded 4–6 px, minus B's dilated hull, and "B-certain" mirrors it. "Neither" covers everything outside both dilated hulls **plus every occluder pixel** from the edge-novelty object cut and XSeg. Build the relief as `edge(x)·exp(−d²/2σ²)`, where d is the distance to the predicted occluding contour (the front face's landmark outline inside the back face's hull) and σ is about 4–8 px. The watershed ridge then snaps to the strongest edge near the predicted contour rather than to any edge. One refinement of my own: add a small prior-only term to the relief so that, where the skin shows no edge at all, the seam falls back onto the landmark contour instead of the midpoint between markers. The output is a label map {A, B, none}. With `α_A = 1[L=A]` and `α_B = 1[L=B]`, the alphas never sum above 1. Feather only a 1–2 px band, with the guided filter or FaceFusion's `(blur.clip(0.5,1) − 0.5)·2` remap, which keeps the 0.5 iso-line in place ([face_masker.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facefusion/facefusion/master/facefusion/face_masker.py)). Then renormalise so that `α_A + α_B` equals the union alpha. Because occluders are seeded as "neither", a hand or cup crossing the seam is cut from both faces on the frame it appears, and the final paste is `own_i·(1 − occ_i)`.

Colour belongs in a supporting role. Per-person Gaussian mixtures in Lab are cheap to score but cost 35–45 ms to fit **[measured]**, so fit them at contact onset, or every 15–30 frames, from uncontested cheek and forehead pixels. Their weakness is structural **[unverified reasoning]**. Lighting moves pixels within one face, lit side against shadow side, by more than the difference between two similar-toned people. The contact zone is usually in shared shadow, where both models are weakest. So add the likelihood ratio to the relief only where it is decisive, for example a mean above 2 nats over a region. Colour does help when there is a real contrast: a beard against smooth skin, lipstick on one person, or one person's hair across the other's face.

### Heavier solvers and learned models belong to contact keyframes

Escalate to GrabCut only when the watershed seam strays more than about 6 px from the landmark prior, or when edge support along the seam is weak. Run one iteration on the band ROI, initialised from a mask with A-certain as hard foreground and B-certain plus "neither" as hard background ([OpenCV segmentation docs](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d3/d47/group__imgproc__segmentation.html)). OpenCV's GrabCut accepts no custom unary terms, so the shape prior can enter only through the probable-foreground and probable-background initial labels. Random walker returns per-label probabilities with `return_full_prob=True` ([scikit-image](https://scikit-image.org/docs/stable/api/skimage.segmentation.html)). That is a useful soft confidence map for keyframes, but at 27 ms it is not a per-frame tool. Geodesic splits behave much like the edge-snapped watershed at roughly 500 times the cost, so drop them.

Learned instance models are either coarse or slow on CPU. YOLO26n-seg runs at **53.3 ms CPU ONNX** with mask mAP 33.9, on an unnamed CPU ([Ultralytics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ultralytics/ultralytics/main/README.md)). It segments whole persons at reduced mask resolution, which is worth something only as a body-level prior in a hug. Multi-person parsers lose accuracy as overlap grows ([arXiv 2509.10093](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.10093)). Promptable SAM-family models are the best learned option. EdgeSAM ships separate ONNX encoder and decoder files, takes negative points (label 0) and positive points (label 1), and scores 42.2–42.7 COCO mask AP against SAM's 46.1 ([EdgeSAM README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chongzhou96/EdgeSAM/master/README.md)). The recipe is to crop tightly around both faces, run one encoder pass, then decode twice. Prompt each face with positives on its landmark-certain points and negatives on the partner's. Enforce exclusivity afterwards with the order decision. On a Qualcomm NPU the encoder took about 81 ms and the decoder 11 ms ([onnxsim PR #1876](https://github.com/onnxsim/onnxsim/pull/1876)). On 2 x86 vCPUs it is likely several hundred milliseconds **[unverified]**, so run it at contact onset and every 10–30 frames of long contact. Video propagation does not fit at all. A third-party SAM 2.1 Hiera-tiny distillation reports **1956 ms per frame** for the teacher on 6 CPU threads and 398 ms for a 576-px mobile variant on 2 threads ([sam2-lite](https://huggingface.co/Pablopigue/sam2-lite)). The practical substitute is a keyframe mask, warped by DIS flow and re-snapped each frame by the watershed. BiSeNet has no "other person" class, so it cannot split two faces. Its nose and lip labels still make near-certain markers, and its hair label inside A's crop flags B's hair crossing A's face. Whether xseg_2 marks another person's face as an occluder is unknown and must be tested on the project's kiss clips.

### Front and behind must be judged per segment, because kisses interleave

A single global order fails in a kiss. A's nose can lie over B's cheek while B's lips lie over A's chin. InstaOrder, the largest dataset of instance-wise orderings (about 2.9M pairs), found that occlusion order and depth order are **complementary**, not interchangeable ([arXiv 2111.14562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14562)). That supports treating order as local rather than global. The engine's outline-edge vote is the right core cue: the front face's outline inside the overlap is a real image edge, while the back face's outline there is hallucinated and edgeless. The proposed upgrade **[unverified design]** has two parts. First, normalise each contour's edge strength by the same face's edge strength on its free outline. Second, score ownership separately on 4–6 segments along the overlap, so the seam's owner can switch along its length. Supporting votes cost almost nothing. An XSeg cross-check asks whether A's XSeg calls the overlap face while B's calls it occluder. A colour likelihood ratio on the overlap pixels counts only when decisive. Landmark jitter counts too, because the back face's guessed points jitter more. Monocular depth from Depth Anything V2 Small serves as a contact-onset tie-breaker; it is Apache-2.0 and 24.8M parameters ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2/main/README.md)), but the few centimetres between kissing faces sit near its local noise. Suggested starting weights are edge 0.4, XSeg 0.25, colour 0.15, jitter 0.1 and depth 0.1. Commit a change only after 5–8 consistent frames. Learned boundary-ownership networks such as DOOBNet ([arXiv 1806.03772](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.03772)) and P2ORM ([arXiv 2007.12088](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.12088.pdf)) exist, but no CPU cost or face-contact evaluation was found.

### Carry the seam with flow, smooth it as a distance field

A per-frame loop of about **3–6 ms at 256 px [unverified sum of measured parts]** keeps the seam stable. Warp the previous label map with DIS flow (FAST, 1.23 ms). Erode each warped label 3–4 px to form markers, leaving a ±3 px band around the old seam unlabelled. Let this frame's landmark-certain regions override the warped labels wherever they conflict, which stops flow drift on low-texture skin from accumulating. Re-run the watershed. Then filter the seam as a **signed distance field** rather than a mask. Accept the new field outright where it moved more than about 2 px, which is real motion. Elsewhere blend it at 0.3–0.5 with the warped previous field. This dead band stops one-pixel flicker without lagging real motion. Re-key with GrabCut or EdgeSAM when contact starts, when the seam drifts more than 6–8 px from the prior over a fifth of its length, when edge support collapses, or at a shot cut. When the front/behind order flips, blend the change through the distance field over 3–5 frames. At contact onset and release, ramp the exclusive cut in and out as the overlap crosses its threshold, so the seam never pops. This favours geometry-locked masks for stability, the same choice Deep-Live-Cam made by caching a fixed elliptical blend mask "identical every frame" ([Deep-Live-Cam](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam)).

## Merged boxes and hidden mouth corners, not the swapper, disfigure contact frames

inswapper_128 has no temporal state. Its identity input is `normed_embedding @ emap`, renormalised, and constant for a given source ([inswapper.py](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface/blob/master/python-package/insightface/model_zoo/inswapper.py)). Every flicker or misshapen face therefore comes from the target side: the detector box, the landmarks, the 5-point alignment, or the paste mask. The engine already paints through contact with zero reverted frames on synthetic clips. On real footage, the open risk is shape.

### Detector hygiene is the first defence

SCRFD in InsightFace runs hard greedy NMS at **IoU 0.4** with a 0.5 score threshold ([scrfd.py](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface/blob/master/python-package/insightface/model_zoo/scrfd.py)). FaceFusion adds a second NMS at 0.4, tightened to 0.1 in its multi-detector mode ([face_helper.py](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/master/facefusion/face_helper.py)). In a profile kiss with one head partly behind the other, the lower-scoring face can simply be deleted. The authors of a score-specific NMS put it bluntly: "in the case of overlapped two faces, this simple and crude method will cause missed detection" ([Score-specific NMS](https://dongl-group.github.io/pdf/Score-specific_Non-maximum_Suppression_and_Coexistence_Prior_for_Multi-scale_Face_Detection.pdf)). Soft-NMS decays overlapping scores instead of deleting the boxes and gained about 1 mAP on COCO ([Soft-NMS](https://github.com/bharatsingh430/soft-nms)). The direction matters. Keeping two overlapping faces needs a **higher** IoU threshold (about 0.5–0.6) or Soft-NMS. Lowering it suppresses more. Better still is track-aware NMS **[unverified design]**. Predict each live track's box and five keypoints. Assign raw candidates to tracks by keypoint distance normalised by eye distance, which discriminates better than IoU when heads overlap. Keep the best candidate per track. Treat a candidate as new only if it matches no track.

The main disfigurement path follows from how 2d106det is fed. InsightFace's landmark model crops **centred on the detector box at 1.5× its larger side** ([landmark.py](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface/blob/master/python-package/insightface/model_zoo/landmark.py)). A merged box therefore centres the landmark crop between two heads, and the regressor returns a hybrid shape. A merged box usually shows up as an inflated box carrying keypoints from one face. Cheap plausibility checks catch it **[unverified thresholds]**. Flag box area or aspect more than about 1.5× the track's running median. Flag eye distance over box width outside ±30% of the track median. Flag a nose keypoint outside the eye–mouth quadrilateral, or eyes in the wrong left–right order after de-rotation. Flag a 5-point fit residual more than about 4 MAD above the track's clean median. On a flag, trust the keypoints over the box. Rebuild the box from the keypoints and the track's scale, and re-run 2d106det on the rebuilt crop. That costs one extra landmark pass, about **5.4 ms per face [measured, production profile]** (local: `core_pipeline.py`).

### Landmark regressors always output a face, so their confidence has to be inferred

Benchmarks understate contact failures because regressors are built to produce plausible shapes. On WFLW, mean error on the occlusion subset is only about 18–29% above the full set: ADNet goes from 4.14 to 5.06 and HRNetV2-W18 from 4.60 to 5.42 NME ([ADNet](https://github.com/huangyangyu/ADNet); [HRNet](https://github.com/HRNet/HRNet-Facial-Landmark-Detection)). The tail grows much more. SPIGA's failure rate doubles from **2.08% to 4.48%**, and its 99th-percentile error rises from 13.1% to **16.4% of inter-ocular distance** ([SPIGA](https://github.com/andresprados/SPIGA)). On the 128-px inswapper template, by my calculation, that is about 2.5 px mean, 5 px at P95 and 8 px at P99 per landmark. WFLW's occluders are hands, objects and hair. In a kiss the occluder is another face, whose eyes, nose and mouth are in-distribution, so the regressor can snap hidden points onto the partner's features. Contact errors are therefore likely worse than these benchmarks **[unverified; no face-on-face landmark benchmark exists]**. 2d106det itself is a 5 MB MobileNet-0.5 coordinate regressor with no per-point score, described as "mainly used for static image inference" ([coordinate_reg README](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface/blob/master/alignment/coordinate_reg/README.md)). LUVLi predicts per-landmark visibility and uncertainty ([arXiv 2004.02980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.02980)), but has no CPU-ready release. FaceFusion approximates confidence with heatmap peaks and falls back to the detector's 5 points below a 0.5 score ([face_landmarker.py](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/master/facefusion/face_landmarker.py)). That option does not exist for a coordinate regressor. Three proxies fit a sub-0.1 ms budget **[unverified]**. The first is disagreement between SCRFD's 5 keypoints and the 5 points derived from the 106, which come from different networks and crops. The second is each point's residual after a robust similarity fit to the track's own median shape from clean frames. The third is each point's acceleration relative to its smoothed trajectory.

### Scale should come from rigid points, because mouth corners make faces breathe

InsightFace aligns inswapper's input with an equal-weight least-squares similarity fit on exactly five points ([face_align.py](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface/blob/master/python-package/insightface/utils/face_align.py)). The two mouth corners carry much of the vertical span, so they control scale. By my own calculation on the 128-px template, a 5-px radial error on one mouth corner, about P95 under occlusion, changes crop scale by **+4.6%**, and a tangential one rotates it by 2.7°. Independent 1-px noise on all five points gives 1.9% scale jitter per frame. A kiss purses and protrudes the lips while hiding the mouth corners, so the pasted face pulses even when the head is still. Down-weighting the mouth to 0.1 cuts the bias to 1.5% but raises noise jitter to 2.65%, because the fit then rests on three points about 35 px apart. Robust fitting and temporal smoothing therefore have to come together. The proposed fit **[unverified design]** takes 20–30 rigid points from the 106 set (eye corners, brows, nose bridge and tip) plus SCRFD's five, matched to a per-track reference shape. It runs `cv2.estimateAffinePartial2D` with RANSAC; the defaults are a 3-px threshold, 2000 iterations, 0.99 confidence and 10 refinement steps ([calib3d.hpp](https://github.com/opencv/opencv/blob/4.x/modules/calib3d/include/opencv2/calib3d.hpp)). The inlier transform is then converted back to the canonical 5-point target, so inswapper still sees its template. Smooth log-scale with a lower cutoff than translation, or lock scale to the track's clean-frame median and let it drift slowly. Smooth the transform parameters, not the 106 points. For chunked offline video, a centred window beats any causal filter; 3DDFA_V2's own smoothing demo uses one frame either side ([demo_video_smooth.py](https://github.com/cleardusk/3DDFA_V2/blob/master/demo_video_smooth.py)). For causal use, the 1€ filter starts at a 1 Hz minimum cutoff with β = 0 and is tuned upward ([OneEuroFilter](https://github.com/casiez/OneEuroFilter)). Disney's production face-swapping work credits its temporal stability to landmark-stabilisation refinement, not to the generator ([Disney Research](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/2020/06/29/high-resolution-neural-face-swapping-for-visual-effects/)). On frames the proxies flag, 3DDFA_V2 offers an anatomically consistent fallback. It regresses 3DMM parameters in **2.25 ms at 2 threads** (0.7 ms for the ×0.5 model) ([3DDFA_V2](https://github.com/cleardusk/3DDFA_V2/blob/master/readme.md)). It still needs a box, though, so box hygiene comes first.

Ownership and alignment interact in a useful way. The behind face's landmarks inside the front face are guessed, but those pixels are never pasted, because the ownership map gives them to the front face. What must be right is the alignment of the behind face's **visible** part, and that is what the rigid-point fit protects. The default inswapper paste mask works against this. It is the whole warped square, eroded by about 10% and blurred ([inswapper.py](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface/blob/master/python-package/insightface/model_zoo/inswapper.py)), which spills swapped pixels over the partner whenever crops overlap. The engine's ownership clip already replaces it. Any new code path, such as a fallback paste, must go through the same clip.

### Never revert: gate the output, carry geometry, then fall back to the last good crop

Because the swapper is stateless, the cheapest output gates check geometry. Distrust the raw geometry when the frame-to-frame change exceeds about 3–5% in log-scale or 3° in rotation while the smoothed trajectory is calm; those thresholds follow from the sensitivity figures above. A geometry-consistency gate re-runs 2d106det on the swapped crop and compares it with the target's landmarks, since inswapper preserves pose and expression (about 5 ms). An identity gate embeds the swapped crop and compares it with the source embedding. At **84.6 ms per face for w600k_r50 [measured, production profile]**, run it only on frames the cheaper gates flag. When a frame fails, the fallback order is as follows **[unverified design]**. First, re-run inswapper on a crop built from the carried, smoothed geometry; the source embedding is unchanged, so the output stays on identity. Second, warp the last good swapped crop to the current geometry, for no more than 3–5 frames before expressions visibly freeze. The raw target crop is never composited. No inspected tool has a policy like this. FaceFusion fills only gaps that lie between two detections in its window, copying identity from the nearer anchor; outside a track's span the frame is left unswapped ([face_tracker.py](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/3.9.2/facefusion/face_tracker.py)). DeepFaceLab's answer is manual re-extraction of failed frames ([main.py](https://github.com/iperov/DeepFaceLab/blob/master/main.py)). The engine's carry already exceeds both. What the research adds is explicit stop conditions, each **[unverified]**. Painting ends at a shot cut (no fade, since a fade across a cut shows as a ghost). It ends when the predicted box leaves the frame by more than half its area. It ends when a hold of about 1–2 s passes without confirming evidence, followed by a 6–10 frame fade. It ends when the identity gate confirms a different person for several frames. A low detector score, a merged box or a landmark failure alone should never end a track. Each should only switch it from fresh to carried geometry.

## Open-source tools select faces one detection at a time

FaceFusion 3.9.2 decides who gets swapped in `select_faces` ([face_selector.py](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/3.9.2/facefusion/face_selector.py)). Mode `many` swaps every face that survives sorting and filtering. Mode `one` swaps the first. Mode `reference`, the default, detects faces in a reference frame and picks one by `reference_face_position`. It then swaps **every** target face whose distance to that single reference passes the threshold. The distance is `1 − dot(embedding_norm_a, embedding_norm_b)`, remapped from [0, 2] to [0, 1], and compared with `reference_face_distance`, default **0.3**. That equals **cosine similarity > 0.4**, the same operating point as 3.0.0's raw default of 0.6 ([3.0.0](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/3.0.0/facefusion/face_selector.py); [program.py](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/3.9.2/facefusion/program.py)). The sort order (default large-small, also left-right, top-bottom or detector score) decides what "first" and "position" mean. The gender, race and age selectors are **hard filters** on single-frame labels from the fairface classifier. They keep only exact gender and race matches, and an age match requires the predicted bin to intersect the requested range. Because Python ranges exclude their upper end, the age bins as coded leave ages such as 2 and 9 outside every bin. The filters apply to the reference face too. An `auto` setting copies the largest source face's gender or race, but the guard `if source_faces and face_selector_gender == 'auto' or face_selector_race == 'auto'` binds as `(source_faces and gender) or race`. Several source images are averaged into one identity, and one run applies that identity to every selected target ([face_swapper/core.py](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/3.9.2/facefusion/processors/modules/face_swapper/core.py)). Swapping two people with two sources therefore takes two independent passes, and nothing stops both passes from choosing the same face in a contact frame. That last point is inferred from the code, not documented. The optional tracker added in 3.7 links faces greedily by IoU with a default score of 0.0 and is off by default. Its gap fill interpolates geometry while copying identity from the nearer anchor ([face_tracker.py](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion/blob/3.9.2/facefusion/face_tracker.py)).

The other tools are looser still. Deep-Live-Cam's video "map faces" clusters every target embedding with k-means, choosing k by the elbow of the inertia curve. It then labels each face with its nearest centroid, with **no distance threshold and no per-frame uniqueness**, so two faces in one frame can receive the same source ([face_analyser.py](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam/blob/0bae1a549014d86457548b05a01a3f7682cdda65/modules/face_analyser.py); [cluster_analysis.py](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam/blob/0bae1a549014d86457548b05a01a3f7682cdda65/modules/cluster_analysis.py)). Rope gives each detection its best-similarity slot above a slider defaulting to 55. That equals cosine ≥ 0.10, and the code comments that "detected faces are independent of each other" ([VideoManager.py](https://github.com/Hillobar/Rope/blob/bf17d2305b127dde1c55ffe301c4ee0f32ae220b/rope/VideoManager.py)). Its own help text advises raising the slider "to reduce faces hopping around when swapping multiple people" ([_default_data.py](https://github.com/Hillobar/Rope/blob/bf17d2305b127dde1c55ffe301c4ee0f32ae220b/rope/qt/_default_data.py)). roop returns the first face whose squared embedding distance is under 0.85 ([mirror](https://huggingface.co/spaces/ALSv/fs-ai/blob/main/roop/face_analyser.py)). roop-unleashed adds "All female" and "All male" modes and a 0.65 similarity slider ([mirror](https://huggingface.co/spaces/ko2009/docker/blob/main/roop/ui.py)). Neither official source could be read, so both are mirror-only.

These designs fail in contact for five concrete reasons. Matching is independent per face, so both faces can take one source. NMS can delete one of two overlapping faces. A contaminated embedding either drops below threshold, so the swap flickers off, or drifts toward the other reference, so identities swap. A single misclassified frame removes a face entirely under a hard attribute filter. And greedy IoU tracking cross-links two faces whose boxes overlap. Two ideas are worth borrowing. One is FaceFusion's refill principle: across a gap, interpolate geometry but **copy identity from the clean anchor** instead of re-estimating it. The other is the reference-distance threshold, which maps onto the engine's absolute floor. The engine's relative admission rule (beat every other identified slot by 0.15, confirmed over 2 frames; local: `swap_engine.py`) is already stronger than anything these tools ship. Their attribute selectors have one legitimate descendant: the soft, per-track, target-learned priors described above.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the contact problem. Identity binding during a kiss is not a recognition task at all, because the only evidence available is a crop that resembles both people. The real question is whether the two people traded places during the blackout. That question can only be answered on the clean frames after it, by summed evidence against memories frozen before it, with retroactive relabelling that the chunked pipeline makes free. Disfigurement changes character in the same way. With identity frozen and pixels exclusively owned, the visible damage left comes from geometry. NMS deletes a face, a merged box breeds a hybrid landmark shape, and two hidden mouth corners set the crop's scale. These failures are cheap to detect and cheap to fix with rigid points and track-rebuilt boxes, and none of them needs a heavier model.

The evidence base is thin exactly where this pipeline operates. No published benchmark covers landmark accuracy, embedding behaviour, detector recall or seam quality with one face occluding another. Every threshold here, from the 0.10–0.15 re-binding margin to the 3× attribute separability rule and the 6 px re-key trigger, is a calibrated guess. Three measurements on the project's real kiss and cheek-to-cheek clips would replace guesses with numbers. The first is a per-frame trace of the `s(A,d) − s(B,d)` gap through contact and separation. The second is per-shot skin, gender and clothing separability for each couple. The third is SCRFD recall at NMS 0.4 against 0.6, alongside watershed-seam deviation from the landmark prior. They should come before any spending on SAM-class models.
